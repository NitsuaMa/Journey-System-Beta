MSF - Considerations for Training with Pain
When working with clients who have chronic pain attributable to either
genetic reasons or overuse injuries, the first step is always to ask whether they
have been formally diagnosed and what limitations, if any, their physician has
recommended. This applies to conditions such as arthritis, tendonitis
including golfer’s or tennis elbow, carpal tunnel syndrome, and similar issues.
While we cannot reverse these conditions, we can often improve comfort and
tolerance by strengthening the muscles and joints surrounding the affected
area. Careful strength training increases the capacity of the tissues to handle
load, which can reduce irritation during daily activities. This is an area where
MaxStrength Fitness is uniquely effective, as our equipment allows us to train
safely and precisely in ways that are often only available in a physical
therapy setting.
Our main priority is always to assess tolerance. This means starting with very
light resistance and possibly limiting the range of motion. If the client does not
experience any increase in pain beyond what they normally feel, we can
gradually add resistance and/or range of motion, and reassess. The goal is to
find a level of load that is challenging but does not aggravate symptoms.
Because our machines can be progressed in two pound increments, we can
make very small, precise increases as the client adapts and gets stronger.
The same can be done with the range of motion as well-small changes over
time.
If the client is able to train with only limited use of the affected area, we may
use most machines with either a static hold or timed static contraction (TSC)
approach. As you’ll remember, a static hold involves holding an actual load
in one position, usually near the midpoint in the range of motion. The TSC is a
more conservative protocol where the subject will produce force against a
static movement arm without holding a load. The movement arm is
positioned in the desired location in the range of motion and then an
excessive amount of weight is selected so that the movement arms cannot
be moved. The subject is then instructed through three phases of increasing
effort; 50%, 75%, and ending with an all out, 100% effort (or whatever can be
comfortably sustained). Static execution allows us to strengthen the muscles
around the joint without repeated movement that may irritate the area.
If the affected area happens to be the hands, wrists, or elbows and it is
limiting to the point where we cannot train the upper body, we can work
around it entirely while still delivering a productive workout. Hand pads are
strongly recommended in these situations as they will blunt the pressure felt
from the movement arm. If this is still too painful to endure, we may have to
consider a “hands free” workout. With our full line of selectorized equipment,
we can continue to train the major muscles of the shoulders and torso using
machines such as the Lateral Raise, Simple Row, Pullover, and Chest Flye. All
lower body and trunk machines remain fully available as well. This is an
approach we have successfully used for many years.
The key point for clients to understand is that strength training does not have
to worsen orthopedic conditions. When done carefully and intelligently, it can
increase strength, improve joint support, and help clients feel more capable
and confident in their daily lives. MaxStrength Fitness is well equipped to
accommodate this and most other orthopedic concerns safely and
effectively.
