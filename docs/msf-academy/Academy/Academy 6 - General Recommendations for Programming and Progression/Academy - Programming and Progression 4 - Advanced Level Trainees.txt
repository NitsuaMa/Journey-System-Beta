General Recommendations for Programming and Progression
Advanced Level Trainees
As a client progresses to an advanced level, the instructor may determine to introduce more exercises and begin to build a C routine. And remember, having more exercises isn't necessarily a better workout or program. The Big 5 are plenty and can provide profound benefit for a lifetime. So a C isn't better, or required, or to even be aspired to. It should happen out of necessity and time. And importantly, it should be slowly introduced, one movement at a time, similar to the B routine. Additionally, once the C routine has been fully introduced it should be run consistently for at least 2 consecutive workouts before rotating between A, B, and C. Again, this is to facilitate proficient learning of the exercises with emphasis on mastering the protocol.
Advanced level subjects may have set durations as low as 3 reps but this is rare.  The upper limit should not go above 9 reps in most cases as this would equate to a 3-minute set and would begin to fall outside of the constraints of a high-intensity effort.
In summary, clients should remain on the basic A routine for as long as possible.  Progressing to new routines should only be considered when absolutely necessary and after the subject has shown proficient mastery of the protocol.
