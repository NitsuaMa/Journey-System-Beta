General Recommendations for Programming and Progression
Intermediate Level Trainees
At a certain point of mastery of the basics, the instructor will determine that the subject is ready to learn new exercises beyond the A routine.  For a majority of subjects and based on individual proficiency, the basic A routine should be performed no less than 5 to 7 times before beginning to add new exercises.  To rush into additional exercises almost always shifts the focus away from mastering the movement and control in general.
Adding exercises must be done in a planned fashion so as not to introduce or include too many at once.  Therefore, a second routine (B routine) needs to be created over time.  This will take place over the course of several or more workouts where the new exercises are added to some of the A routine exercises (usually replacing one at a time). Again, our focus needs to be on the quality of the effort not the number of exercises. So take time to spread this out for several or more weeks if you can.  Eventually, there will be enough new exercises introduced and learned to be able to create two separate routines (the original A routine and the newer B routine).
New exercises should be introduced and programmed for several consecutive sessions to help speed up the learning curve.  Remind the subject that the new exercises may not be performed as intensely for purposes of proper learning and we will progress accordingly. Give positive feedback if they seem to grasp the movement quickly and then make sure to consider using a challenging load on the next session. But first they must pass our high standards. Perform the movement perfectly. Especially the changes of direction.
Intermediate subjects will typically have shorter set durations than that of a beginner as the loads used will have increased commensurate with proficiency (expect somewhere between 5 to 10 reps).
