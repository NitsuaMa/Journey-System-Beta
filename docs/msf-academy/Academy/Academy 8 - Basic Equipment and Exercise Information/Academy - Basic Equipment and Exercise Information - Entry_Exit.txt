Basic Equipment and Exercise Information - Entry/Exit
Entry, Exit, Stools
Proper entry and exit of equipment is necessary for both safety and workout efficiency.  Some machine designs present potential tripping or collision hazards which must be avoided.  Some machines may require the movement arm to be raised after the user has entered for the movement arm to be in the proper starting position.  Every machine has a specific entry and exit point as well as a specific method for entry/exit.  Refer to the practical scripts for the detailed analysis on how to properly instruct this topic: https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1f29em_iNFAcRRMwhtqW97VNOtd0Kc3wB?usp=drive_link.
Also of high importance is the need for stools for some clients on certain exercises. This need may arise to simply get into and out of the machine but they may also be required to support or raise the client's legs during exercise. In all cases, the instructor must be aware and attentive to the need for a stool. Of equal importance should be the instructor's proactive placement and removal of the stool in cases where it may be a trip hazard. The client should never have to step over or around a stool. It is the sole responsibility of the instructor to manage the location of the stools at all times to ensure the safety of the clients.
On certain exercises, the instructor may need to raise the movement arm then transfer it to the trainee, which is considered an interpersonal transfer (see the full discussion in Resistance Transfer module: https://docs.google.com/document/d/1dCo7yHoeIGMIwYr3BXZaG9YpUMovFW-BM4gSPhg6kMk/edit?usp=drive_link).
Instructors must know how to safely enter and exit a machine, including how to properly perform an interpersonal resistance transfer where required.
