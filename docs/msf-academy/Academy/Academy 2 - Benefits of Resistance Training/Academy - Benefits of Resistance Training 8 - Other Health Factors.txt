Benefits of Resistance Training
Other Health Factors
Resistance training has also been shown to improve many other health and fitness factors, including blood pressure, resting heart rate, insulin sensitivity, glucose metabolism, cholesterol/HDL ratio, gastrointestinal transit time, the body's ability to regulate its temperature, and decreased discomfort from arthritis.  Improvements in body shape resulting from resistance training and proper nutrition can improve self-esteem and overall psychological well-being.
But one of the more exciting and possibly unexpected findings has come from a review published in the British Journal of Sports Medicine, which represents the most comprehensive to date, that shows that physical activity is extremely beneficial for improving symptoms of depression, anxiety, and distress.  More specifically, the new study shows that physical activity is 1.5 times more effective than counseling or the leading medications[¹].  And while all types of physical activity (e.g., walking, resistance training, pilates, and yoga) were beneficial, higher-intensity exercise had greater improvements for depression and anxiety.
