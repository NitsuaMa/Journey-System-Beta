Benefits of Resistance Training
Why Resistance Training?
Most activities commonly accepted as exercise are only capable of improving one, or at most two factors of fitness.  As a result, many people erroneously believe it is necessary to perform a variety of activities to improve all areas of fitness, including resistance training for muscular strength, “aerobics” for cardiovascular fitness, stability training for balance, and stretching for flexibility.  This approach is time-consuming and potentially dangerous because of the harmful levels of impact force the body is exposed to along with unstable positions during many popular “fitness” activities.
Resistance training is commonly utilized for the purpose of improving muscular strength and size.  Properly performed resistance training is also capable of improving cardiovascular fitness and several other important factors of fitness and health.  Resistance training is the safest, most beneficial, and most time-efficient form of exercise for improving total fitness and impacting longevity. Additionally, it is the best fitness option for the deconditioned, the elderly, and trainees with injuries or health conditions sensitive to physical activity.
Current research, the medical community, and public perception are also finally "catching up" to the idea that resistance training is the superior method of exercise for promoting health and longevity.
Further support for this paradigm shift can be found in any number of daily articles, medical journals, news headlines, and podcasts.  One such research paper appeared in a 2022 publication of the scientific journal, Cells, titled Molecular Mechanisms of Exercise and Healthspan. The paper's authors provide us with an encouraging and informative summary statement, "Healthspan is the period of our life without major debilitating diseases.  In the modern world where unhealthy lifestyle choices and chronic diseases taper the healthspan, which leads to an enormous economic burden, finding ways to promote healthspan becomes a pressing goal of the scientific community.  Exercise training remains the most potent 'medicine' that preserves quality of life and expands healthspan.  The molecular understanding of exercise's impacts on different organ systems reinstates that exercise is the most powerful lifestyle intervention against chronic diseases.  While human lifespan seems to approach its limit, great potentials lie in promoting physical activities among any given communities to improve the healthspan and possibly lifespan as well."
And quite possibly the most exciting area of resistance training benefits research lies in the further understanding of myokines.  As published in a 2022 International Journal of Molecular Sciences, "In the last few years, the muscular system has gained attention due to the discovery of the muscle-secretome and its high potency for retaining or regaining health.  These cytokines, described as myokines released by the working muscle, are involved in anti-inflammatory, metabolic, and immunological processes.  These are able to influence human health in a positive way and are a target of research in metabolic diseases, cancer, neurological diseases, and other non-communicable diseases. Therefore, different types of exercise training were investigated in the last few years to find associations between exercise, myokines, and their effects on human health. Particularly, resistance training turned out to be a powerful stimulus to enhance myokine release."[¹]
In yet another enlightening article from the peer-reviewed academic journal, Endocrine Reviews, the following benefits of myokines were noted:
"Physical activity decreases the risk of a network of diseases, and exercise may be prescribed as medicine for lifestyle-related disorders such as type 2 diabetes, dementia, cardiovascular diseases, and cancer. During the past couple of decades, it has been apparent that skeletal muscle works as an endocrine organ, which can produce and secrete hundreds of myokines that exert their effects in either autocrine, paracrine, or endocrine manners. Recent advances show that skeletal muscle produces myokines in response to exercise, which allow for crosstalk between the muscle and other organs, including brain, adipose tissue, bone, liver, gut, pancreas, vascular bed, and skin, as well as communication within the muscle itself."[2]

Myokines: The Hidden Messengers of Muscle
Muscles do more than just move your body—they are also powerful endocrine organs that release signaling molecules called myokines whenever they contract. Think of myokines as tiny messengers that travel through your bloodstream, communicating with other organs and tissues to improve your overall health.
Research has shown that myokines have a wide range of positive effects:
Anti-inflammatory effects: Myokines help reduce chronic inflammation, which is linked to many age-related diseases, including heart disease, diabetes, and arthritis.
Metabolic support: They improve how your body processes and uses nutrients, helping with blood sugar regulation, fat metabolism, and overall energy efficiency.
Organ communication: Myokines interact with your brain, liver, bones, and other organs to optimize function and resilience. For example, certain myokines help protect the brain and may support cognitive health as you age.
Immune system benefits: By modulating immune activity, myokines contribute to better defense against illness and help your body recover more efficiently.
The exciting part? Resistance training, particularly high-intensity strength work like the MSF Continuous Tension Protocol, is one of the most powerful ways to stimulate myokine release. Every rep you perform sends these signals throughout your body, improving not just your muscle strength but your health systemically.
From a client perspective, this means that your workouts aren’t just making you stronger—they are actively helping your body combat chronic disease, maintain metabolic balance, and improve long-term health. In other words, the effort you put into lifting isn’t just about looking or feeling stronger in the moment—it’s about creating lasting, systemic benefits that protect and enhance your body for decades to come.
