Exercise Performance
Warm-up Considerations
For general exercise, a separate warm-up set is unnecessary.  Assuming an appropriate level of resistance, the first several repetitions provide all the warm-up necessary for the exercise.
By initially fatiguing the target musculature we prepare it to safely perform maximum effort later in the same set.  A muscle is less likely to be able to injure itself in a fatigued state as its force capability is greatly diminished well below the tensile strength of the tendons and ligaments.  Again, this assumes proper behavior and execution during the entire exercise.
Also, consider that the mechanical action of the exercise allows for the membranous fluids to flow more freely and lubricate the muscles and joints. For these reasons, the most direct and efficient approach to warm-up is performing the exercise itself.  Of course, the exception to this would be a subject with a joint debility where a lighter exercise to work the joint might be performed prior to a more challenging effort (e.g. leg curl preceding a leg press for a knee replacement recipient).
