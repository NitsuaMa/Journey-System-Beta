Exercise Performance
Number of Sets - Single Set Principle
In most cases, only one set is performed per exercise.  The majority of research shows no significant benefit for most people to perform more than one set of an exercise as long as that set is taken to or close to muscular failure.  This is the best way to ensure that the stimulus created from the exercise is recoverable and emphasizes the ideal that the quality of the effort is paramount.  If one is to perform only a single set of an exercise, one must utilize every bit of focus and intensity that can be called upon.
Exceptions to this may include certain rehab situations or for severely deconditioned or extremely intensity adverse clients.  In these cases, multiple sets performed at a sub-maximal level of effort may be beneficial.  In rare cases, a rest-pause may be used for more experienced trainees that may have just “mistimed” their effort.  Importantly, it is the responsibility of the MSF Exercise Practitioner to ensure a productive workout for the client.  In the event that an experienced client is just having an “off day” or has just lost focus for a moment and prematurely ended their set, a second attempt may be performed.  This second attempt should be employed only after a sufficient rest period where the client is able to catch their breath and consideration should be given to reducing the weight.  It is also imperative that this approach does not become a regular occurrence or used as a crutch in place of proper focus and technique.  But in rare situations, it may help to ensure the client is satisfied with their effort and the workout.
