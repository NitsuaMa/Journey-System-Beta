Continuous Tension Method
About our Continuous Tension Method
In setting out to develop our training protocol, safety was the primary concern.  Therefore a slower, smoother movement pace that minimizes momentum and sudden forces was adopted.  A slower, continuous speed of movement will also ensure a more orderly recruitment and sustained overlapping of muscle fibers (actin and myosin cross-bridges).  This creates the possibility for more mechanical tension due to the force-velocity relationship, which dictates that a muscle fiber exerts higher levels of force at slower contraction speeds.
Our execution of directional changes is of even greater importance under our methodology.  An exaggerated control with a high level of focus is required to ensure continuous movement with no pause (from eccentric to concentric) or acceleration.  While this does make for a more technically challenging execution that requires a higher level of motor control, it more importantly creates a safer approach to strength training.  It is often the change of direction in an exercise (and sports for that matter) where dangerous sudden forces are introduced.  Along with the gradual load up, if a subject attempts to precisely control the changes of direction, they will accomplish a majority of what it takes to properly perform our Continuous Tension Protocol.
This careful control combined with a high degree of effort is a challenging but rewarding endeavor for both the client and the instructor.  The Exercise Practitioner and client will grow together in their expertise and abilities making this a lifelong learning process full of enriching experiences along the way.   The trainee becomes more trusting of and reliant on the trainer to ensure progress and the trainer becomes more insightful and appreciative of the trainee's accomplishments.
