Leg Press - Custom gap – lay back in seat, starts at LT
SETUP
Set back pad (P2 for most), set shoulder pads, set weight, move seat back
“Have a seat, feet on frame below [point], butt back, lay back in the seat.”
“This exercise targets your thighs/quads and your glutes.  You’ll also be using your hip muscles and hamstrings too as this movement recruits most of the lower body muscles.”
Prepare them to be able to reach the platform with their feet by moving their seat forward to a temporary staging position.  BUT they should still have their feet down on the frame at this point.
“I’m going to slide you forward some but please keep your feet down where they are for now.”
Position yourself to be able to push the footplate out to remove the slack caused by the gap.  KEEP HOLD OF THE FOOTPLATE UNTIL THEIR FEET ARE PROPERLY POSITIONED.
“I’d like you to place your feet up on the platform so that they are hip width apart and parallel to each other.”  (NOTE: As discussed previously there will be exceptions to this foot placement as some clients with larger midsections and/or those that lack strength to keep the femur internally rotated may benefit from a slightly wider and externally rotated [toes out] stance).
Ensure feet are positioned symmetrically and at a reasonable location.  Be consistent with where they place them vertically as this will change the result of the established settings (seat and gap).  In general feet towards or near the bottom will most likely accommodate everyone unless there are knee issues.  This will be noted on the chart as well.
“Now keep your feet on the footplate but try not to push as I am going to slide you forward into position.”
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK…hands on the handles [either set], looking straight ahead…establish your breathing and keep breathing as you begin to ‘grad-u-ally’ apply pressure to the footplate.”
Observe closely, watching for a proper gradual load up.

“Yes!  Now, SLIGHTLY speed up in order to stay smooth.  Keep that same pace.  Remember there are no pauses on a LP…it’s one continuous effort.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
UPPER TURN
“Be ready to touch the end stop and immediately change directions as if you’re passing right through it…good, [click], ease out..don’t speed up.”
Watch closely to ensure the concentric has been fully completed.  Client should reach the end stop (UT) and instructor must be ready to ‘click’ at that exact moment.
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Head still…breathing freely…keep that pace.”
“Now as we approach the bottom of the movement, you are going to barely let those plates touch and seamlessly change directions.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
“Perfect!  Make it look the same every time!”
The client will be inclined to look at the weight stack on every LT, although not ideal, it’s probably fine on the first rep or two, after that and as they become more advanced they should not rely on this.
LC pg 1 of 2
Leg Curl - Gap 3 for most – starts at LT, chest up
SETUP
Set seat, set weight, set calf pad
Bring movement arm down to allow client to sweep their legs in.
“Have a seat with your legs angled out to the side…now sweep your legs in between the pads.”
Seat belt
“We are targeting the hamstrings and calf muscles on this movement.”
Ensure legs are symmetrically aligned in the pads, with knees at the top of the roller pad and pointing up.
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK…from here…looking straight ahead, chest up, hands on the handles, ankles relaxed, establish your breathing and gradually begin to bend your knees and dent the roller pad.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Good…now stay smooth, pulling your heels back toward your rear end.”
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing…see if you can get even tighter at the knee joint …good…now, stay tight…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has fully exhausted their ROM.  Some clients will be able to pull the movement arm back to its furthest point, while others will have what appears to be a limited ROM.  Wherever that range of motion ends is where the pause/squeeze will take place and where we will consider a full rep.  Just ensure that if there is an apparent lack of ROM that it is not from an apprehension to contract (close the knee joint further) or from excessive weight.
(NOTE: This is the “squeeze” technique that we refer to.  Recall that on the first two reps there is a brief pause of 1 to 2 seconds.  From the third rep on, there is a “squeeze” of 2 to 3 seconds.  The squeeze is just a slightly longer pause.  It’s used to establish control and to help create awareness of the working muscles.  The same cueing can be used for both the pause and the squeeze.  The duration of the pause on the first two reps is shorter with shorter phrasing.  The instruction on the squeeze is slightly longer with slightly drawn out instruction.)

LE pg 1 of 2
Leg Extension - Gap 2 (3 or 4 for knee issues) – starts at LT, chest up
SETUP
Set seat, set weight, lift the movement arm out of the way with the assist handle so that the client may sweep their legs in behind it.
“Have a seat, butt back”
Begin to carefully lower the movement arm, watching for the roller pad to approximate the bottom of the shin.
“Now we want this pad to land at the bottom of your shin just above your foot.  How does that feel?”
Seat belt
“This exercise targets your thighs/quads.  You’ll most likely feel this right above the knee joint.”
“Lay back in the seat keeping your head forward.  Hands on the handles at your sides…you are going to be pulling UP on them for leverage.” (NOTE: This is a common missing component for optimizing this exercise).
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK…from here…chest up, head still, breathing freely, pulling up on the handles, begin to gradually apply pressure through the shin pad as you begin the movement toward straightening the knees.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Mmm, hmm…good…now, SLIGHTLY speed up to stay smooth…maintain that pace.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing, keep your head still, see if you can get even straighter at the knee…good…lock it in place…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has reached their fullest possible ROM.  Encourage them to “straighten further” or “straighter, straighter.”  Knee extension will vary dramatically between individuals.  Some will appear to almost over-straighten while others may not even come close.  Ensure that a lack of ROM is not from a lack of willingness to produce more effort or from excessive loading.

ADDuction - Custom gap – starts at LT, laying back in seat
SETUP
Set seat back (6 or 7 for most), set the accessory stack, remove main stack pin and secure movement arm closest to the weight stack with your knee at an intermediate position.
“Have a seat, butt back, legs down in front of you.”
Bring movement arms in closer with the knee so client can reach them with their legs.
“I’d like you to lift your left leg and place it into the movement arm.”
Stay attentive to any difficulty that may have posed the client as they may require assistance.
With the client’s left leg in place, bring the movement arms closer together to allow client to position their right leg.
“Now, lift your right leg for me and position it in the machine.”
With both of the client’s legs now in place and instructor still controlling movement arms with their knee, begin to lower the weights allowing the clients legs to drift apart until we have reached the desired gap.  BE CAUTIOUS AND OBSERVANT OF THE TOLERANCE OF THE CLIENT.  EVEN THOUGH THEY HAVE ESTABLISHED SETTINGS, THEY MAY FEEL VULNERABLE IN THIS POSITION.
“I’m now going to slowly lower down into the starting position.”
Pin the main stack at the desired gap.
“...feel ok here?...good…this exercise will target the large area of muscle on the inner thigh.”
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“From this position, lay back in the seat keeping your head forward, hands on the handles, establish your breathing, and gradually apply pressure to the pads as you begin to bring your knees together.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Mmm, hmm…good…now, SLIGHTLY speed up to stay smooth…maintain that pace.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
ADD pg 2 of 2
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Breathing freely, head still, bringing your knees together…the effort ONLY comes through the knees, the feet and ankles are relaxed…good…now clamp down…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has in fact brought the movement arms together and that there is no ‘fluttering’.  We’re looking for absolute stillness in the contracted position.
Make sure the effort comes through the knees (femur) and not the lower legs or feet.
(NOTE: This is the “squeeze” technique that we refer to.  Recall that on the first two reps there is a brief pause of 1 to 2 seconds.  From the third rep on, there is a “squeeze” of 2 to 3 seconds.  The squeeze is just a slightly longer pause.  It’s used to establish control and to help create awareness of the working muscles.  The same cueing can be used for both the pause and the squeeze.  The duration of the pause on the first two reps is shorter with shorter phrasing.  The instruction on the squeeze is slightly longer with slightly drawn out instruction.)
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Keep this pace…same speed in both directions.”
“Now, we want to be seamless at the bottom…touch and go smoothly.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
“Yep, driving through the knees…stay smooth.”

ABDuction - No gap – starts at LT, chest up
SETUP
Set seat back (6 or 7 for most), set the weight, set the thigh pads.  Ideally the thighs will be snug/compressed while still allowing the weight stack to sit flush at the LT.  (NOTE: Clients with hip replacements may start with a wider position).
Grab the bottom of the end of the movement arm closest to the weight stack (the inside edge of the pad).  Pull the movement arms open.  (NOTE: If the movement arm is too heavy to move when loaded, trainer will have to set the weight after client is seated.  Keep in mind the movement arms will have to be carefully forced together compressing the legs some as they will naturally push them apart at rest).
“Have a seat, butt back, and lift your legs please, onto the movement arms.”
Lower the movement arms down slowly to facilitate the client’s lifting and loading of their legs.
Have the client shift to get settled between the pads and check the pads for congruency/comfort of the femurs.
“This exercise will target your hips and butt muscles.”
Seat belt
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“From this position, lay back in the seat, chest up, head forward, hands on the handles, establish your breathing, and gradually apply pressure out into the pads as you begin to drive your knees apart.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Mmm, hmm…good…now, SLIGHTLY speed up to stay smooth…maintain that pace.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they may need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing…chest up…SMOOTHLY drive those knees apart as far as you comfortably can…good…now lock it in place…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has reached their furthest ROM and that there is no ‘fluttering’ or ‘rebound’.  We’re looking for absolute stillness at the furthest point reached (NOTE: ROM will vary considerably between clients).

Any sudden or jabbing movement to produce an increased ROM will immediately be followed by a loss of ROM or ‘rebound’.  Be attentive to this and correct it.
(NOTE: This is the “squeeze” technique that we refer to.  Recall that on the first two reps there is a brief pause of 1 to 2 seconds.  From the third rep on, there is a “squeeze” of 2 to 3 seconds.  The squeeze is just a slightly longer pause.  It’s used to establish control and to help create awareness of the working muscles.  The same cueing can be used for both the pause and the squeeze.  The duration of the pause on the first two reps is shorter with shorter phrasing.  The instruction on the squeeze is slightly longer with slightly drawn out instruction.)
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Don’t speed up…keep this pace.”
“Now, we want to be continuous at the bottom…touch and go smoothly.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
