Chest Flye – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Pectoralis Major (Chest/Pecs): Shoulder horizontal adduction
Synergists
Anterior Deltoid (Shoulder): Shoulder horizontal adduction
Considerations for Setup
Determine seat height based on subject height: taller subjects generally need a lower seat, shorter subjects a higher seat
Fine-tune seat height so upper arm (humerus) slopes down slightly from shoulder to elbow, or is at least level to the floor in starting position
Humeral head should be slightly above or level with elbow when hands are on handles
Subject’s head should align near the padded “work box” overhead when seated
Theoretical alignment: shoulders under machine’s axes of rotation (determined by back pad position)
Practical alignment takes priority: elbows must remain beyond and in contact with roller pads throughout entire ROM
For longer arms, set back pad further from roller pads; for shorter arms, set back pad closer to roller pads
Ensure roller pads do not hit the client during movement (back pad too far forward) or lose contact with the elbows (back pad too far back)
Consider both arm length and torso size to determine optimal back pad position
Adjust to create an appropriate gap at the lower turnaround for a conservative stretch
Ideal starting position: elbows slightly behind the midline of the torso (frontal plane) when viewed from the side
For subjects with shoulder issues, use a larger gap and reduce stretch to accommodate tolerance
Posture / Get Set
Chest up, butt back, feet on stool
Chin slightly tucked (neutral)
Arms behind roller pads, hands on handles
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Load Up: From lower turnaround, maintain chest-up posture, neutral head position, arms in place, establish breathing, gradually drive forward through elbows
Instructor quietly observes; use cues like “mmm, hmm” or “yes, just like that” for positive feedback if performed properly
Concentric / Upper Turn: Client drives elbows together until roller pads make contact; maintain chest-up posture throughout
Hold contracted position for 1 second on initial reps or 2–3 seconds from third rep onward
Instructor clicks at end of desired pause/squeeze to begin eccentric
Eccentric: Immediately cue with “ease out, keep that pace” or equivalent
Lower Turnaround: Cue client with phrases like “touch and go,” “be continuous at the bottom,” “barely touch, barely start,” “seamlessly change direction as the weights touch,” or “soft touch, do not pause”
Lower turnaround must be continuous and controlled; avoid bottom pausing to adhere to Continuous Tension protocol
One continuous pace throughout set
Pause for ~1 second at contraction on first two reps, squeeze 2–3 seconds on subsequent reps
Avoid vague cues like “squeeze”; provide clear instruction on how to contract
Example verbiage:
“Keep your chest up and gently bring those pads together… now keep breathing and continue to drive through your elbows, attempting to further dent those pads… keep still… [click], ease out… don’t speed up”
Maintain posture, head position, consistent pace, and continuous contact with arm pads
Only full reps reaching proper contraction are counted toward total rep number
If full ROM is no longer achievable but control remains, instructor may allow continuation of partial reps for stimulative tension
Decision to extend set beyond concentric failure is individual; consult studio Leader or MSF Corporate as needed
For new clients, completing a few full reps may be sufficient; failure is not required if discomfort, form degradation, or lack of readiness is evident
Effort must increase during the set to maintain consistent pace under increasing fatigue; this creates effective mechanical tension
Notes
Always point out overhead padded mechanism on entry/exit; say “watch your head” and gesture to ensure safety
If client has short arms and cannot reach handles without creating an upward humeral angle, have them grip the top of the roller pads instead
Chest Flye is effective for clients with wrist, hand, or elbow issues who still need chest stimulation
Works well as a chest-focused supplement and can be paired with:
Overhead Press in one routine
Chest Press in an alternating routine
Allows for twice-weekly chest stimulation without overloading arms or grip
During early stages of learning Continuous Tension protocol, client will likely use light weights and complete more reps
Excessive time under tension from high rep counts may lead to discomfort before reaching stimulative levels
Progress to heavier weights (~10 reps max) with controlled 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric cadence once form is consistently demonstrated
