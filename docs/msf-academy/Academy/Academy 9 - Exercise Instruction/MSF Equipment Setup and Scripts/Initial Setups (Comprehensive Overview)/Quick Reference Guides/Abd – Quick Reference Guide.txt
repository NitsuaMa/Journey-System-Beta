Abduction – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Gluteus Medius (Glutes/Hips) – Hip horizontal abduction
Synergists
Gluteus Minimus (Glutes/Hips) – Hip horizontal abduction
Gluteus Maximus – lateral fibers (Glutes/Hips) – Hip horizontal abduction
Piriformis – Hip horizontal abduction
Considerations for Setup
Seat back is typically standardized at position 6 or 7 to maintain a more upright position
Thigh pad width determines starting point and lower turnaround, thus a gap is never needed
Pads should be as snug as possible without discomfort; slight compression of soft tissue may be desirable for increased ROM
If client has a history of hip issues or reports discomfort, slightly wider pad settings may be warranted
Subjects with larger hips/legs typically require wider pad settings; smaller individuals narrower settings
Movement arms must be pulled apart manually during client entry
If weight is too heavy to allow arms to be pulled apart manually, wait until client is seated to load weight stack
When setting weight post-entry, assist movement arms fully closed to allow selector pin placement; take additional care due to soft tissue compression
Posture/Get Set
Chest up, butt back, seat belt secured around waist (not over femurs or restricting hips), head neutral, feet and ankles relaxed
Hands on handles for balance—not for anchoring
Encourage anterior pelvic tilt facilitated by upright chest and slight lumbar arch
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Load Up
From lower turnaround, cue client to lift chest, maintain neutral head, hands on handles, and gradually apply outward pressure through the knees
Instructor observes closely during initial load; brief affirmation such as “good” or “yes, just like that” as positive feedback
Concentric Phase / Upper Turnaround
Client drives knees apart “as far as comfortably possible” while lifting chest and arching back
Brief 1-2 second pause on first two reps and a 2–3 second squeeze from third rep onward
Instructor clicks at precise moment duration of squeeze is completed
Eccentric Phase
Click is immediately followed by instruction to “ease out, do not speed up”
Cue first lower turnaround before it is reached with language such as “touch and go,” “be continuous,” or “seamlessly change directions”
Always provide a cue on the first eccentric phase of the set to establish expectation for lower turn
Lower Turnaround
Movement should be continuous at the bottom with no pause—“touch and go”
Maintain chest up by leaning back and using handles for leverage (pushing down, not pulling up to anchor)
Execution
One continuous pace throughout
First two reps should have a brief 1-2 second pause in contracted position
Reps three and beyond include a 2–3 second squeeze at top
Verbal cues should guide HOW to squeeze, not just “squeeze”
Example verbiage:
“Lift your chest, arch your back, continue to drive out through your knees as if the movement continues…keep breathing, keep still, [click], ease out”
“Keeping your head still, drive your knees apart as hard as you can…there, lock it in place…breathe freely…[click], ease out”
Only full reps (full contraction) are recorded on client charts
Partial reps may still be useful for stimulus if performed under control and subject is advanced/tolerant, especially near failure
For new clients, full ROM for a limited number of reps can be sufficient stimulus without going to failure
Encourage consistent pace with increasing effort to offset fatigue and to maintain tension
Notes
Watch for pinch points when loading/unloading clients; pull movement arms apart by ends of padding and return slowly as client lifts legs
Ensure client hands are clear of rocking/tilting thigh pads
Expect variability in client range of motion; encourage full ROM through safe abduction
Use first 2–3 reps to assess individual ROM and adjust coaching accordingly
Ensure limited ROM is not due to excessive weight or poor execution understanding
Instruct clients to drive through knees even if pads are not contacting them directly
Driving through knees is simpler to coach than through the femur, though the femur is the actual lever
Clients with hip replacements or other hip pathologies may not tolerate abduction
Conservative approach is to omit the exercise entirely or implement Timed Static Contraction (TSC) protocol
TSC Protocol:
Pin weight too heavy to move with movement arm near midpoint
Instruct client to press outward statically:
30 sec @ 50% effort
30 sec @ 75% effort
30 sec @ 100% effort
Performed at least weekly over months, this builds meaningful strength and potential for dynamic reintroduction
Expect carryover of ~15 degrees in either direction from static point
Progression Considerations
While learning Continuous Tension, lighter weight may result in higher reps and longer time under load
This may become counterproductive due to discomfort before stimulus is achieved
Over time, strive for perfect form with a slightly heavier load (goal: failure in ≤10 reps)
Target 6-second positive, 6-second negative reps
If subject demonstrates appropriate control and understanding, intensity can be increased sooner
