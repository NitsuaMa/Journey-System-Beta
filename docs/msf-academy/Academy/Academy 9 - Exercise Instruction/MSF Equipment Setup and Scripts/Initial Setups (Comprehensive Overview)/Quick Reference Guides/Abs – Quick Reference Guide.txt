Abs – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Rectus Abdominis – Spinal Flexion/Posterior Pelvic Tilt
Synergists
External Obliques – Spinal Flexion
Transverse Abdominis – Hollowing (supports abdominal wall)
Pelvic Floor – Pelvis stabilization (in coordination with TA)
Rectus Femoris – Hip Flexion
Psoas – Hip Flexion
Considerations for Setup
Set the weight stack prior to client entry; a standard gap of 6 is typical but may vary by machine and location as determined by Studio Leader and MSF Corporate
Ensure the movement arm (roller pad) is upright and locked; it only rests forward and will fall forward if not secured
Instructor must control the movement arm throughout the set, especially during client entry and exit
Determine seat pad height beginning with aligning the client’s iliac crest with the top of the roller pad at the back of the seat
Practical alignment must consider client height (torso length) and size of hip girdle; consider if they are too far above or below movement arm pad
Move femur restraint away from the seat to allow for client entry/exit
Instruct client to avoid contact with the movement arm when entering; keep arm in locked position and hold it securely in rear position during entry
Set foot placement on the fixed footplate to approximate knee angle with the femur restraint (knee pad) angle
With feet on the platform, adjust femur restraint so that pads straddle the knee caps evenly
Femur restraint should rest snugly above and below the knee, flush against femur and tibia, with slight wiggle room and no gaps
Unlock movement arm and carefully lower it into position in front of client once client is seated
Instruct client to lean back and lift arms over roller pad as it comes down; pad should rest in armpits or as close as comfortably possible
Client should bring elbows together and place hands in front of head in a “prayer” position
Start movement in the upper turnaround (contracted position) with instructor assisting client and movement arm into position
Instructor cues client to tighten abdominals and then gradually transfers control of the load while keeping the movement arm stationary
Posture/Get Set
Instructor assists client and movement arm into contracted position
Client increases abdominal effort while instructor gradually releases assistance, ensuring control throughout the transfer
Instructor gives a cue like “thaaat…iiisss…yyyours” to signify load transfer completion
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Eccentric Phase
Instruct client to “slowly begin to lower the weight by uncurling the spine from the bottom up”
Cue client before reaching lower turnaround to “uncurl far enough to allow the weights to barely touch before changing directions and curling/tightening back down”
Always provide a cue on the first eccentric phase of the set to establish expectation at lower turn
Lower Turnaround
Before weights touch, cue client to “immediately” or “seamlessly” reverse direction once they do touch
“Immediate” indicates prompt transition, but not a sudden one
Concentric Phase
Instruct client to “begin to curl back down one vertebrae at a time from the top down toward their lap (or hip crease)” or “begin to draw their rib cage down to their pelvis”
Cue client to “tighten your abdominals and round your back…” to describe anatomical intent
Upper Turnaround
Concentric movement ends when abdomen is fully contracted—not necessarily when the movement arm reaches the top of the legs
Emphasize that goal is contraction of targeted musculature, not full range of motion of the machine
Brief pause at upper turnaround on first two reps, with a 2–3 second squeeze beginning with the third rep
Verbal cues for squeeze: “keep rounding, keep breathing, [click], ease out” or “tighten down hard, lock it in place, [click], ease out”
Always describe HOW to “squeeze” as it may not be instinctive for most
Notes
Use consultation paperwork and client interview to assess tolerance for trunk flexion
Omit abdominal machine for clients with orthopedic restrictions until medically cleared
For some with issues on this exercise dynamically, a conservative strategy is to perform a Timed Static Contraction (TSC):
Pin weight too heavy to lift with movement arm near midpoint
Lead client through progressive static effort:
30 seconds @ 50% effort
30 seconds @ 75% effort
30 seconds @ 100% effort
Perform at least weekly for long-term strength gains and potential dynamic progression
Expect carryover benefit of approximately 15 degrees in either direction from held position
If TSC is not feasible, consider substituting with Torso Rotation to benefit abdominal musculature without trunk flexion
Machine’s resistance profile is heavier at the lower turnaround and lighter at the top
Contracted position may be used instinctively as a rest point by clients; avoid this by placing a selector pin in an upper stack pin hole to truncate end range (UT)
If truncation strategy is ever to be used, instructor must test it personally and document setup clearly in client chart
