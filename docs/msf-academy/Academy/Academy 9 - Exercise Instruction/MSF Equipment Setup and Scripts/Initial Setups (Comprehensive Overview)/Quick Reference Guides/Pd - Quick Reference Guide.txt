Pd - Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles:
Latissimus Dorsi (Shoulder Extension, Sagittal Plane, Supinated)
Teres Major, Posterior Deltoid, and Biceps (Elbow Flexion, Supination)
Synergists:
Rhomboids (Scapular Downward Rotation)
Lower Trapezius (Scapular Depression)
Pectoralis Major and Triceps (Shoulder Extension)
Erector Spinae and Rectus Abdominis (Spinal/Pelvic Position)
Wrist Flexors - gripping
Considerations for Setup:
Provide stool if client requires assistance for entry or foot support
Handles in supinated position so the ends face in toward each other and slightly forward (one notch toward neutral)
Confirm subject is seated behind the vertical path of the handles (upper arm’s length)
Use back pad setting of 2 or 3 for most; 1 for larger subjects needing deeper seat placement
Avoid using seat positions 1-4 as that tends to be too high for shorter, more deconditioned clients to safely reach
As a result, using only seat positions 5-9 will require a larger gap of the weight stack
With subject seated bring handles down to them so that there is still a slight bend in the elbow and pin the weight stack and thus setting the gap
Ensure weight stack does not bottom out too soon; the subject’s arm should almost but not quite straighten at the lower turn (i.e. there should still be a slight bend in the elbow at the lower turn)
Grip should be shoulder-width or narrower to achieve narrow arm path for optimal lat engagement
Posture/Get Set:
Cue chest up, hips back, chin slightly tucked, upper back in contact with seat back
Secure seat belt tightly
Provide stool option if legs are unsupported/uncomfortable or entry is difficult
Assist client in pulling handles toward contracted position near eye level
Perform controlled handoff using verbal cue (e.g., “that is yours”)
Confirm client establishes control before beginning eccentric
If movement occurs during handoff, cue “increase your force” or “pull harder”
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds:
Begin with handles near eye level using trainer assistance
Cue “slowly begin to let the arms straighten” for first eccentric
Once arms almost straighten, the weights should lightly touch, instruct immediate and controlled bending of the elbows (lower turnaround)
Weights usually touch at lower turnaround unless intentionally permitted to fully straighten without weights touching for purposes of a stretch
Full contraction occurs when elbows are pulled into the sides of the rib cage, not beyond the rib cage (upper turnaround), wrists should remain flat
Optional variation: cue abdominal contraction for added engagement at upper turnaround
Do not allow subject to lean forward or elbows to move behind torso during contracted abdomen variation
Ensure client does not over-pull or excessively flex wrists in an attempt to keep moving until the “click”
Click only after observing control and full ROM at upper turnaround with brief pause in contracted position
Cue examples: “Drive elbows into the sides of your ribcage… there… [click] ease out”
Maintain posture, head position, consistent pace, and arm path throughout set
Intensity:
End set at true concentric failure with perfect form
Record only full, controlled reps achieved
Allow for partial reps beyond midpoint only with experienced clients demonstrating control and competency under duress
Avoid partials for newer or deconditioned clients; focus on consistent, full ROM
Emphasize progressive effort with perfect form throughout the set to overcome accumulating fatigue
Reinforce that effort must increase to maintain pace and produce meaningful stimulus
Notes:
Torso Arm setup uses neutral grip and frontal plane movement (as opposed to sagittal plane with supinated grip)
Pulldown biases thoracic/lumbar divisions; Torso Arm biases iliac division of lats
Use Torso Arm for subjects that cannot tolerate supinated pattern.  This may be for any number of reasons including hand/wrist issues, shoulder pain, elbow valgus, etc.  Sometimes just making a slight modification can make for a more tolerable outcome.  Torso Arm may also be a good form of variation for a more advanced client that has been with us for years.
Do not restrict natural scapular movement unless excessive shrugging is observed.  The scapulae should naturally draw down with the arms
Prioritize stabilization of head and ribcage over scapular positioning
Handles should be reachable for assisted start and self-managed finish
Address valgus elbow issues with ROM modifications, pushing movement first, or grip variation
Avoid prolonged light-load sets in early stages; increase load and reduce time once control is evident
Ideal early progression: heavier load, 6–10 reps with 6-second tempo on both concentric and eccentric
This will be highly individual and some clients will need to stay with a lighter weight for higher reps even though it is not ideal
