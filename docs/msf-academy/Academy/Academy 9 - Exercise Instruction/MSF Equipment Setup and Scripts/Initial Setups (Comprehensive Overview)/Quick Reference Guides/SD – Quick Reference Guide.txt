Seated Dip – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Pectoralis Major (Costal and Sternal Heads): Shoulder flexion from hyperextension, horizontal adduction
Triceps: Elbow extension
Anterior Deltoid: Shoulder flexion
Synergists
Erector Spinae: Spinal extension, anterior pelvic tilt (chest-up posture)
Lower Trapezius: Scapular stabilization
Serratus Anterior: Scapular depression and protraction
Latissimus Dorsi: Assists in shoulder flexion from hyperextension to anatomical position
Rotator Cuff: Shoulder stabilization
Considerations for Setup
Most clients will require a stool to support the legs due to high seat position; some may also need a stool for entry
Seat height determines both the starting position and the intersection of hands with movement arms, affecting shoulder/elbow angles (lower turnaround)
Shorter clients will need a lower seat to more safely enter/exit the machine.  In this case, there will likely be a gap to bring the handles down lower
Aim for upper arms abducted 45–60 degrees; elbows bent ~90° or more based on mobility
Humerus/elbow will likely be behind torso in starting position depending on seat and back pad setting; this tends to be a more shoulder friendly movement pattern than a Chest Press and can therefore allow more ROM at LT
Hands should be placed more forward on handles in starting position; establish consistent placement for all clients
Allow client to choose either neutral or pronated grip; pronated grip involves “capping” the ends of the handles; most seem to prefer pronated
Use Fat Gripz to distribute pressure and improve comfort; subject pushes through heel of palm rather than gripping
Favor a higher seat setting for safety to avoid excessive stretch of shoulder capsule and hyperflexion of elbows for those with shoulder issues
Adjust seat depth based on body size: smaller subjects may sit more forward, larger subjects more back; most will not use entire seat
If hands slip forward, adjust by moving forward in seat or repositioning hands slightly back on handles; consider hand pads
Set back pad angle and lower to meet mid/upper back to provide leverage; this promotes forward lean and will act to increase stretch at chest and shoulders
Adjustments may be necessary post-loading due to compression of padding and changes in spacing
Posture / Get Set
Raise seat back to allow proper entry into seat
Fasten seat belt tightly to counter upward force from movement
Once belted, lower seat back to desired setting (see above)
Set posture: chest up, hips back, head neutral (downward gaze due to forward lean), shoulders retracted, hands on handles
Upper arms abducted 45–60 degrees; elbows bent 90° or more
Feet must contact a stool for stability and comfort; ball of the foot at least
Hands placed at/near end of movement arm (preferably Fat Gripz); choose neutral or pronated grip
Begin movement at lower turnaround; no gap necessary on this machine
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Start from lower turnaround with rigid posture: chest up, shoulders retracted, chin tucked, upper arms abducted
Instruct gradual pressure through heel of palm while  breathing
Observe quietly at the beginning of the load-up; offer simple positive reinforcement if correctly executed
Ensure subject is leveraging off back pad
Movement arms travel down as subject attempts to:
Straighten arms fully
Protract shoulder blades
Contract abdominals
These actions should occur synergistically to complete the upper turnaround
Instructor must watch for full contraction before clicking to indicate upper turnaround
Emphasize that clicking occurs only after movement stalls, not when handles are pushed as low as possible
Reinforce the goal is to fully contract—not just move handles further
Sample verbiage:
“Drive your arms straight, and tighten down hard on the abdomen, keep breathing, [click], ease out”
“Keep straightening, keep breathing, round your back hard into the back pad, [click], ease out”
After first concentric and click, establish expectation for lower turnaround:
“Touch and go” / “Be continuous” / “Do not pause” / “Barely touch, barely start”
Maintain continuous pace throughout the set, with brief pause only as needed to ensure full contraction
Continue cycle until concentric movement stalls (muscular failure)
Maintain posture, head position, and consistent elbow tracking throughout (path)
Effort must increase to match rising fatigue in order to maintain pace and produce stimulative tension
Notes
Explain gradual load-up to client if needed:
“20 lbs of pressure, 40 lbs, 60 lbs…” or “Pretend there's 100 lbs, find 101 lbs of pressure”
Load-up may take 3–5 seconds before movement occurs
No pause allowed during Seated Dip—movement must remain continuous
Emphasize controlled changes of direction: “drag out the turn” or “almost pause but don’t”
Ensure subject reaches full contraction before clicking for direction change
In early stages of learning MSF protocol with lighter weight, client may perform higher reps which will likely create discomfort well before stimulative reps
Over time, ideally progress to heavier loads (leading to failure ≤10 reps) once subject demonstrates control and acceptance of intensity
Form and understanding should guide intensity progression, not just tolerance to discomfort
