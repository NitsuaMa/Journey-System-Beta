Basic Principles of Exercise
Specificity
Adaptations to exercise are specific to the type of stimulus the body encounters.  This is known as the SAID (Specific Adaptation to Imposed Demand) principle in physical therapy and sports training.  Resistance training not performed intensely enough, in a manner that imposes overload on the cardiovascular system, will not stimulate improvements in cardiovascular efficiency.  Exercises performed for the muscles of the frontal thighs will not directly stimulate improvements in the strength of the shoulder muscles.
Exercise selection and method of performance should be specific to the types of adaptations desired.  While a general program of high-intensity training produces overall improvements in all aspects of physical fitness, it is possible to alter the programs to target a particular muscle group or other aspects of fitness.  And while strength remains our main focus, fatigue resistance or cardio can be emphasized by shortening rest periods between exercises.  Or by running several compound movements sequentially in a workout.  While this is not necessarily optimal for strength building, it is an example of specificity and how workout parameters can be adjusted to target different mechanisms.
