# Standardized Baseline Setup Guide (Batch 1: First 5 Machines)
### Onboarding Protocol for New Clients (Continuous-Tension, High-Intensity Training)

This guide provides the standardized baseline starting points and body-type variables for onboarding new clients on the first 5 machines of the Imagine Strength and studio lineup.

---

## 1. Compound Row

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Rhomboids, Trapezius (scapular retraction/adduction, downward rotation), Latissimus Dorsi (shoulder extension, adduction) [151, 204].
*   **Secondary Muscles**: Posterior Deltoid, Teres Major, Biceps Brachii, Brachioradialis [151, 152, 204].
*   **Synergists**: Erector Spinae (spinal stabilizers), Wrist Flexors (grip support) [152, 205].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat & Chest Pad Height**: Set based on the client's torso length, ensuring the chest pad is positioned so the handles align directly with the chest [152].
*   **Chest Pad Depth**: Position the chest pad so that the neutral handles are just within the client's fingertip reach with arms fully extended and shoulders rounded forward slightly [152, 205].
*   **Axis of Rotation Alignment**: Confirm that the overhead movement arm mechanism will clear the top of the client's head during entry, exit, and throughout the full range of motion [152, 206, 213].
*   **Restraint & Handle Settings**: Default handle width is set to the middle position ("M") [153, 206]. The chest pad must be positioned so that the client can maintain a slight forward lean into the pad [153, 207].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 2 [152, 205].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set chest pad height to the "down" position [152, 205].
    *   Set the chest pad closer to the handles to accommodate a shorter reach [152, 205].
    *   Use the narrow handle setting ("N") to match a smaller skeletal frame and keep the arm path correctly aligned [153, 206].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set chest pad height to the "up" position [152, 205, 216].
    *   Set the chest pad further back from the handles [152, 205].
    *   Use the wide handle setting ("W") if the client has a wider build [153, 206].
    *   *Caution*: If the overhead mechanism is too close to the client's head, move the chest pad further back and increase the weight stack gap [153, 206].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Sternum Sensitivity / Large Abdomen*: Adjust chest pad height or lower it to minimize uncomfortable pressure against the sternum, or raise it to accommodate a larger abdomen [159, 216].
    *   *Valgus Elbow (Elbow Discomfort)*: A shortened range of motion that limits full elbow extension is beneficial [215]. If discomfort persists, switch the client to the pronated handles, which require a wider arm path and elbow drive [159, 215].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Hip Position & Torso Lean**: Verify that the client's hips are not tucked under/against the chest pad. The hips must remain back with a slight forward lean [153, 207]. Leaning away from the chest pad increases spinal extension contribution from the lower back, which is undesirable [207].
2.  **Overhead Clearance**: Confirm the overhead movement arm mechanism is completely clear of the top of the client's head on entry, execution, and exit [153, 158, 206, 213].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up**: The movement requires an instructor-assisted handoff as the set begins near the contracted position (just beyond the midpoint, as the client's leverage is poor and they cannot hold the fully contracted position alone initially) [154, 155, 207, 209]. The coach assumes a wide base, grabs the bottom of the movement arm with one hand and the pronated handle with the other, pulls back with the client, and transfers the load by cueing, *"That is yours"* once control is established [154, 208, 209].
*   **Continuous Tension Cadence**: Command a slow, smooth 6-second eccentric (letting the arms straighten) and a controlled 6-second concentric (elbows driving back) [160, 209, 216]. 
*   **Turnaround Protocols**: 
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless change of direction with no pause at elbow extension [155, 210]. Weights do not typically touch, but may touch to protect a valgus elbow or ensure consistent ROM [155, 210].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Pause in the fully contracted position (elbows passing the torso or aligned with the side of the ribs) [155, 156, 210, 211]. Implement a 1-2 second pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [155, 156, 211, 212]. Click only after visual confirmation of full contraction and control [156, 211].
    *   *Turnaround Cue*: *"Draw the shoulders back," "Pull with your shoulder blades,"* (upper back-biased) or *"Pull your elbows into your sides"* (lat-biased) [156, 212].

## 2. Chest Press

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Pectoralis Major (chest/pecs - shoulder horizontal adduction), Triceps Brachii (elbow extension), Anterior Deltoid (shoulder horizontal adduction) [147, 192].
*   **Synergists**: Erector Spinae (spinal stabilizers), Lower Trapezius (scapular stabilizer), Rotator Cuff (shoulder stabilizers) [147, 192].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set so the client's upper arm is abducted approximately 30–45° off the body, the hands align with the handles, and the elbows remain slightly lower than the hands throughout the movement path [147, 193].
*   **Seat Back Position**: With the weight stack clear, assist handles to the converging upper turnaround [194]. Adjust the seat back so that the client can just fully straighten the elbows without protracting their shoulders or rounding the chest forward [147, 194].
*   **Pad Placement & Restraints**: Standard footstool must be provided for all clients to support the legs and position the knees higher than the hips [147, 192, 197].
*   **Grip & Hand Position**: Place the heel of the palm on the bend of the handle to create a semi-pronated grip with the thumb side slightly rotated upward [147, 194, 406].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Establish the lower turnaround starting point using a custom gap (assisted back to a conservative, reasonable stretch) [147, 195].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Raise the seat height to maintain correct elbow alignment (elbows slightly below hands) [150, 193, 202].
    *   Move the seat back/back pad forward [150].
    *   Provide assistance and a stool for entry/exit if the seat is too high [147, 192].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Lower the seat height [150, 202].
    *   Move the seat back/back pad further back to accommodate longer arms [150, 202].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Compromised Shoulder Joint*: Increase the weight stack gap (shallower lower turnaround), ensuring the client's elbow remains forward of the midline of the torso when viewed from the side, which limits the deep shoulder stretch [147, 195].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Forearm Path & Joint Stacking**: Verify that the forearm is aligned with the movement path of the machine. The elbows must remain slightly lower than the hands and track on the same out-and-up trajectory as the handles [147, 150, 193, 202].
2.  **Scapular Retraction & Foot Anchor**: Ensure shoulders are held in retraction with the chest up, and the feet are firmly planted on the stool so that the knees are higher than the hips [147, 148, 196, 197]. No dangling feet are allowed, as this leads to pelvic shifting and back instability [148, 197].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround (stretched position) [147, 148, 198]. Cue a gradual, patient load-up (building pressure over 3–5 seconds, imagining finding 101 lbs of pressure to crack a 100 lb stack) [148, 150, 198, 201]. Once moving, slightly speed up to stay smooth, and maintain a strict 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric pace [150, 203, 407].
*   **Continuous Change of Direction**: As a compound pushing movement, there is **no pause or squeeze** at either turnaround [149, 199, 201].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Converge handles fully and click at the exact moment of full elbow extension (without shrugging or shoulder protraction) [147, 149, 194, 199]. Click timing is critical; immediately cue *"ease out... do not speed up"* post-click [149, 199].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Drag out the turn to change direction seamlessly. Barely touch the plates and "touch and go" without pausing [149, 199, 201]. Cue: *"Barely touch, barely start"* or *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch"* [149, 199].

## 3. Leg Press

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Quadriceps (thighs - knee extension), Gluteus Maximus (rear end/buttocks - hip extension) [246, 290].
*   **Synergists**: Hamstrings, Gastrocnemius, Soleus, Adductor Magnus [246, 290].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back Position**: Set to Position 2 (P2) as the default optimal starting point [246, 290].
*   **Shoulder Pads**: Set so they make snug contact with the tops of the shoulders when the client is fully reclined in the seat [246, 291].
*   **Foot Placement**: Position feet parallel and hip-width apart on the footplate to ensure ankles, knees, and hips are perfectly aligned ("stacked") [246, 291].
*   **Seat Settings**: Slide the seat so the footplate abuts the end stop just before the knees fully straighten (always maintain a slight knee bend; never lock out) [246, 292]. Account for spacing created under load by pushing the seat a setting or two closer than what appears necessary on initial setup [246, 292].
*   **Weight Stack & Restraints**: Pin the accessory stack at 18 lbs to facilitate setup and stabilize foot placement with 38 lbs of total passive pressure (20 lbs main + 18 lbs accessory) [246, 291, 292]. Pin the main stack to establish and record the starting gap [246, 293].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Push the seat settings closer to the footplate [246, 292].
    *   Use a vertically lower foot position on the platform to accommodate shorter legs [299].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Recline the seat back to Position 3 (P3) to reduce midsection congestion and knee/hip flexion joint angles [246, 290].
    *   Set the seat settings further back [334].
    *   *Stance Adjustment*: For clients with larger frames or limited internal rotation of the femur, take a wider stance on the platform with the toes angled slightly out, ensuring the knees track over the feet at that same angle [246, 291].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Hip Flexion / Lower Back Sensitivity*: If the client does not tolerate deep hip flexion or experience lower back discomfort, increase the weight stack gap to create a shallower lower turnaround [249, 303]. If sensitive, avoid pairing Leg Press with the Lumbar Extension machine in the same workout (or place several exercises between them) [249, 303].
    *   *Knee Constraints*: Position the feet higher on the footplate to decrease knee flexion (biasing hip extension/glutes) [299, 302].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Knee Tracking**: Verify that the knees track in a direct line with the parallel feet, maintaining stacked ankles, knees, and hips during both concentric and eccentric phases [246, 291, 297].
2.  **Pelvic Stability**: Watch closely at the lower turnaround to ensure the hips remain firmly down and the pelvis does not roll up or lift the tailbone out of the seat [248, 297].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround starting point [294, 334]. Cue "chin down" (neutral head position) and establish breathing [247, 294]. Patiently build pressure through the footplate over 3–5 seconds to crack the weight stack [249, 294, 298]. Slightly speed up once moving to avoid segmentation, and maintain a smooth 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric cadence [249, 301, 337].
*   **Continuous Motion**: As a compound pushing movement, there is **no pause or squeeze** at any point during the movement [247, 248, 295, 298].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Click at the exact moment the end stop is reached (fully converged just before lock-out) [247, 295, 338]. Immediately cue *"ease out... do not speed up"* to begin the eccentric smoothly [248, 296, 338].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly reverse direction without momentum or pausing [248, 296]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change direction"* or *"Barely touch, barely start"* [248, 296, 338].

## 4. Leg Extension

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Quadriceps (all four heads are active, most notably the Rectus Femoris, which is not stimulated from pressing movements like the Leg Press) [234, 271].
*   **Synergists**: Brachioradialis, Wrist Flexors (gripping and pulling up on handles to anchor hips into the seat) [234, 271].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back & Axis Alignment**: Move the seat back to the rearmost position [235, 271]. Have the client sit and align their knee joint with the machine's axis of rotation [235, 272]. Bring the back pad forward to make contact with the pelvis/lower back without pushing them forward of the axis of rotation [235, 272].
*   **Tibia Pad Placement**: Adjust the tibia pad so that it makes flush contact with the subject's shin just above the top of the foot [236, 273, 344].
*   **Restraint Settings**: Securely fasten the seat belt tightly across the subject's hip crease (ensuring it does not pull down on or restrict the femurs) [237, 275].
*   **Leverage Handles**: Instruct the client to grab the side handles and actively pull UP on them throughout the set to leverage the torso down and isolate the quadriceps [238, 275, 345].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 2 due to deep knee flexion [234, 236, 271, 273].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Lower the seat position (use seat positions 5–9 to make entry/exit safer) [235, 236].
    *   Increase the gap on the weight stack to lower the tibia pad and handles within a reachable range [236, 237].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Use the rearmost seat settings [235, 271].
    *   Ensure the knees are not too close to the edge of the seat, which can cause excessive, uncomfortable pressure behind the knees [272].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Knee Injuries / Pain*: Increase the gap setting to 3 or 4 to avoid deep, pain-producing knee flexion at the starting lower turnaround [236, 243, 273].
    *   *Conservative Static Protocols*:
        *   *Timed Static Contraction (TSC)*: For compromised joints, set a heavy, unmovable weight. Position the movement arm at the midpoint (biased slightly toward extension). Lead the client through a 90-second static contraction (30s at 50% effort, 30s at 75% effort, 30s at 100% effort) [243, 244, 287].
        *   *Static Hold (SH)*: Load a conservative weight and insert the upper selector pin above the stack to create a hard end stop. Assist the client into a static hold for 30–45 seconds, gradually progressing to a 120-second maximum [244, 288].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Knee-Axis Alignment**: Confirm the center of the client's knee joint is in direct lateral alignment with the machine's axis of rotation. The knees must not be pushed forward of the axis [235, 272].
2.  **Hips Anchored (Active Pulling)**: Visually verify that the client's pelvis remains firmly anchored back into the seat. If the client fails to actively pull UP on the side handles, their hips will lift during full knee extension, reducing the tension on the target muscles [239, 275, 277].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround starting point [238, 275]. Ensure the face, jaw, feet, and ankles are relaxed [238, 275]. Patiently build pressure into the tibia pad over 3–5 seconds (cue: *"denting the shin pads"*) to crack the stack [241, 275, 281]. Once moving, slightly speed up to stay smooth, and maintain a 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric tempo [245, 289, 345].
*   **Turnaround Protocols & Ankle Cues**:
    *   *Relax the Feet/Ankles*: Explicitly instruct the client to relax their feet and ankles throughout the set [241, 275, 282]. Natural instincts to dorsiflex cause calf (gastrocnemius) tightness, which inhibits full quadriceps contraction and knee extension [241, 282].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Revert direction seamlessly with a continuous "touch and go" (no pausing) [239, 277]. Cue: *"Be continuous at the bottom, do not pause"* [239, 348].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Fully straighten the knees and pause [238, 239, 276, 277]. Implement a brief 1-2 second pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [239, 277]. 
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Pull up on handles, fully straighten your knees, keep still, breathe freely"* [239, 278]. Click only after movement stops and maximum extension is achieved [238, 276].

## 5. Pullover

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Latissimus Dorsi (Lats - shoulder extension) [385, 450, 483].
*   **Synergists**: Teres Major, Posterior Deltoid, Rhomboids, Trapezius, Pectoralis Major, Triceps Brachii (long head), Rectus Abdominis (abdominal wall / thorax stabilizer) [385, 450, 451, 483].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back Position**: Set to the "up and back" position for most clients [451, 484].
*   **Seat Height & Axis Alignment**: Adjust seat height so that the machine's axis of rotation is slightly above the center of the shoulder joint when the client is seated with arms at their sides [451, 485]. This accounts for the shoulder joint's natural upward and downward movement path [451, 485].
*   **Arm Pads**: Middle ("M") setting [385, 452, 486]. Elbows must land at least halfway down the pads and maintain a 90-degree angle throughout the range of motion without sliding along the pads [452, 487].
*   **Restraint Settings**: Securely fasten and tighten the seat belt tightly across the lap to counter the powerful upward reactionary forces [385, 487, 500].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 6 to establish a conservative overhead stretch [385, 452, 487, 493].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set the seat back to the "down and forward" position [451, 484].
    *   Set a higher seat height [451, 484].
    *   Use the narrow arm pad setting ("N") to match a smaller torso width [452, 486].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a lower seat height [451, 484].
    *   Use the wide arm pad setting ("W") if needed for torso width [452, 486].
    *   Maintain the default "up and back" seat back position [451, 484].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Shoulder Joint Limitations*: If the client has restricted shoulder mobility or joint discomfort, increase the weight stack gap to limit the overhead stretch [452, 487].
    *   *Elbow Angles / Sliding*: If the elbows slide or the elbow angles bend/straighten beyond 90 degrees during execution, adjust the seat height [452, 487].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Shoulder-Axis Midpoint**: Confirm the machine's axis is slightly above the shoulder joint center at rest. This represents the optimal "average" midpoint of the shoulder's natural excursion during the pull [451, 485].
2.  **Elbow-Driven Load (No Hand-Pushing)**: Ensure the elbows are bent at 90 degrees and positioned at least halfway down the elbow pads [452, 487]. Hands must rest lightly on the bar—no grabbing, gripping, or hand-pushing is allowed [386, 453, 488].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up**: The movement requires an instructor-assisted handoff as the set begins in the fully contracted position (upper turnaround) [385, 454, 490]. Establish a posterior pelvic tilt (PPT) to contract the abdomen and round the back [453, 488]. Pull the movement arm down, cue the client to place their elbows on the pads, hands resting lightly on the bar, and drive back through their elbows while tightening the abdomen. Hand off the load using the verbal cue: *"Driving back through your elbow, tighten your abdomen, that... is... yours"* [386, 454, 489]. Ensure absolute stillness and control before starting the eccentric [454, 489].
*   **Postural Phase Shifts**: Note that posture naturally changes between phases on this exercise: the ribcage rises (anterior pelvic tilt) during the eccentric as arms raise, and the abdomen tightens, drawing the ribcage down (PPT) during the concentric [456, 492].
*   **Continuous Tension Cadence**: Command a controlled 6-second eccentric (letting the movement arm rise while maintaining pressure through the elbows) and a smooth 6-second concentric (elbows driving down) [455, 459, 490, 495].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless change of direction as soon as the weights touch (no pausing) [386, 455, 490]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change directions"* [386, 455].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Fully contract the lats by drawing the elbows into the sides of the ribs and tightening the midsection [455, 491]. Implement a 1-2 second pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [387, 491, 492]. Click only after confirming full ROM and control [456, 491]. Squeeze cue: *"Draw the elbows down into the ribs... abdomen tight, elbows in, keep still"* [387, 492].
