# Standardized Baseline Setup Guide (Batch 3: Machines 11-15)
### Onboarding Protocol for New Clients (Continuous-Tension, High-Intensity Training)

This guide provides the standardized baseline starting points and body-type variables for onboarding new clients on the third batch of machines from the Imagine Strength and studio lineup.

---

## 11. Leg Curl

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Hamstrings (Knee Flexion), Gastrocnemius (Calf - Knee Flexion) [224, 250].
*   **Synergists**: Sartorius (assists in knee flexion), Gracilis (assists in knee flexion) [224, 250], Wrist Flexors (anchoring into seat with arms) [224, 250].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back & Axis Alignment**: Move the seat back to the rearmost position to maximize the seat's usable surface area [225, 251]. Have the client sit and align the lateral center of their knee joint with the machine's axis of rotation [225, 251, 252]. Bring the back pad forward to make light contact with the lower back without pushing their knees forward of the axis [225, 252].
*   **Calf Pad Placement**: Adjust the calf pad to make snug contact with the lowest part of the back of the leg, positioned just above the Achilles tendon (above the heel) so that it does not contact the shoe [226, 256].
*   **Restraints & Handles**: Securely fasten the seat belt tightly across the hip crease [227, 257]. Instruct the client to grip the side handles firmly, pulling UP on them throughout the set to leverage the hips down into the seat [227, 257].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Establish a standardized starting gap of 1 or 2 to accommodate the initial knee flexion starting position and avoid knee joint hyperextension [224, 253].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Bring the seat back pad further forward to maintain proper pelvis contact, ensuring the knees remain strictly aligned with the axis of rotation and do not slip forward of the seat edge [252].
    *   If the client is seated with higher seat adjustments, instruct them to rest their feet back on the toes of their shoes for initial loading if necessary [251].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Use the rearmost seat settings [225, 251].
    *   Clients with larger legs and hip girdles require more room and a larger starting gap (such as 2 or 3) at the starting point to avoid potential hyperextension at the knee [253].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Knee Injuries / Sensitivity*: Increase the starting weight stack gap to 3 or more to avoid deep, pain-producing knee flexion or hyperextension at the starting turnaround [224, 250].
    *   *Limited Knee Flexion ROM*: If the client is physically unable to fully bend their knees, redefine their full range of motion on the chart ("limited ROM" or "limit flexion") [232, 268].
    *   *Hard End Stop (Upper Turn)*: For clients lacking full knee flexion, place the extra selector pin in the main stack pin above the weight stack so that it abuts the frame at the client's approximate terminal concentric contraction [232, 268]. This establishes ROM consistency and provides a solid target to squeeze against [232, 268].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Knee-Axis Congruency**: Confirm that the lateral center of the client's knee joint is aligned with the machine's axis of rotation [225, 252]. The patellas must "crowd" the roller pad with the top of the tibia under the pad, but no part of the patella may be tucked under the roller pad, which puts the knee in a vulnerable, painful position [225, 251].
2.  **Pelvic Anchoring**: Watch for the client's hips lifting out of the seat during deep flexion [229, 259]. The pelvis must remain firmly anchored back into the seat, secured by a tight seat belt and active, continuous upward pulling on the handles [227, 229, 257, 259].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround (knees extended) with feet and ankles relaxed [227, 257]. Patiently build pressure into the roller pads over 3–5 seconds (cue: *"gradually bend your knees and dent the roller pad"*) to crack the stack [230, 257, 264]. Once moving, maintain a smooth 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric cadence [230, 233, 270].
*   **Ankle Toggling Protocol (Critical Technique)**: 
    1.  Keep the feet and ankles relaxed upon load-up and during the first two repetitions to isolate the hamstrings [227, 257, 265].
    2.  From the third repetition onward, instruct the client to actively dorsiflex their ankles during the concentric phase (cue: *"pull your feet up to your knees"*) to engage the gastrocnemius (calf) for added mechanical leverage and a tighter contraction [228, 259, 265].
    3.  At the upper turn (fully contracted position), instruct the client to relax their ankles *before* initiating the eccentric phase (cue: *"relax the ankles, THEN ease out"*) [229, 260, 265]. Relaxing the ankles must take place while the weight arm is still, before beginning to lower the weight [260, 265].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change directions with no pause ("touch and go", "barely touch, barely start") [228, 239, 259, 343].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Implement a brief 1-2 second pause in full contraction on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [229, 260, 342]. Click only after visual confirmation of full contraction and control [229, 260].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Pulling up on the handles, drive your knees into the roller pad while pulling your heels toward your rear end, keep breathing, keep still... [click], relax your ankles, THEN ease out, don't speed up"* [229, 261].

## 12. Simple Row

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Posterior Deltoid (Rear Delt - shoulder horizontal abduction), Rhomboids (scapular retraction/adduction, downward rotation), Trapezius (scapular retraction/adduction, downward rotation) [506, 531].
*   **Synergists**: Infraspinatus (rotator cuff), Erector Spinae (spinal stabilizers) [506, 531].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Adjust seat height so that when the client sits with their hands on the handles, the upper arm (humerus) slopes down slightly from the shoulder to the elbow (humeral head slightly higher than the elbow) [138, 507, 532]. The top of the head should end near the padded "work box" overhead [139, 507, 532].
*   **Chest Pad Depth**: Adjust the chest pad so that when sitting upright, the client's elbows land in at least the middle of the concave arm pads on the movement arm [507, 533]. The abdomen must be in flush contact with the chest pad [509, 536].
*   **Handles & Hand Placement**: Adjust vertical handle height so that elbows align with the center of the arm pads [508, 535]. Hands should be placed on the handles to create a force couple with the elbow [508, 535].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Typically, no gap is used on the Simple Row as the movement starts at the lower turnaround with the client's arms forward [396, 536].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set a higher seat height to maintain the slight downward humeral slope [507, 532].
    *   Move the chest pad in closer to the arm pads [508, 534].
    *   For shorter legs/higher seats, rest the feet back on the toes of the shoes for stability [509, 536].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a lower seat height [507, 532].
    *   Set the chest pad further out away from the arm pads [508, 534].
    *   Slide the vertical handles upward to ensure the elbows align with the center of the arm pads when arms hang from the handles [508, 535].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Limited External Rotation*: If the client has restricted shoulder rotation or discomfort, instruct them to "cap" the ends of the handles with palms facing themselves (pronated grip), which provides a more comfortable shoulder path [509, 535].
    *   *Shoulder Joint Issues*: If the client cannot tolerate dynamic horizontal abduction, perform a Timed Static Contraction (TSC) [514, 543]. Pin a heavy weight to prevent movement, position the movement arms at the midpoint of the range of motion, and guide them through a 90-second static contraction (30s at 50% effort, 30s at 75% effort, 30s at 100% effort) [514, 543].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Elbow-Pad Alignment**: Confirm the client's elbows land squarely in the center of the concave arm pads, and verify that the upper arm pads remain flush and congruent against their humerus throughout the entire movement [507, 508, 533, 535].
2.  **Abdominal Bracing (No Leaning Back)**: Verify that the client's abdomen remains in contact with the chest pad [509, 536]. The torso must not lean back away from the pad to advance the handles, as this unloads the posterior deltoid/upper back and engages the lower back extensors [513, 536, 542].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround (extended position) with chest up [509, 536]. Establish a patient 3-5 second gradual load-up (cue: *"gradually apply pressure by driving out through the elbows"*) to crack the stack [510, 537]. Once moving, maintain a smooth 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric cadence [515, 544].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change directions with no pause ("touch and go", "seamlessly change directions") the instant the weights touch [511, 538].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Lift the chest and draw the shoulder blades together to achieve full retraction [510, 537]. Implement a brief 1-second pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [511, 539]. Click only after visual confirmation of full contraction and control [510, 537].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Leveraging off your abdomen, lift your chest and draw your shoulder blades together as hard as you can... keep breathing, keep still... [click], ease out, do not speed up"* [511, 539].

## 13. Seated Dip

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Pectoralis Major (Costal and Sternal Heads - shoulder flexion from hyperextension, horizontal adduction), Triceps Brachii (elbow extension), Anterior Deltoid (shoulder flexion) [496, 516].
*   **Synergists**: Erector Spinae (spinal stabilizers), Lower Trapezius (scapular stabilizers), Serratus Anterior (scapular depression and protraction) [496, 516].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set seat height so that the upper arms are abducted approximately 45–60 degrees, and the elbows are bent at approximately 90 degrees or more in the starting lower turnaround position [498, 519, 522].
*   **Back Pad Placement**: Set the back pad angle forward and lower it to meet the client's mid-to-upper back, promoting a forward torso lean and providing leverage to oppose the upward reactionary forces [521, 522].
*   **Feet Support**: Provide a standard stool for all clients to support the legs, ensuring the knees or at least the ball of the foot rests firmly on the stool to prevent leg dangling [497, 501, 517, 523].
*   **Restraints & Grips**: Securely fasten the seat belt tightly across the hip crease to counter the powerful upward reactionary forces [500, 522]. Place hands near the ends of the handles (preferably with Fat Gripz to distribute pressure) and instruct the client to push through the heel of the palm rather than hard gripping [499, 519, 524]. Neutral or pronated grip may be selected [498, 519].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Typically, no gap is used on the Seated Dip as seat height determines the lower turnaround [518].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Shorter clients should sit more forward on the seat to accommodate a shorter torso [412, 520].
    *   Use a lower, closer-to-the-floor seat setting (seats 5–9) to make entry/exit safer [497, 517, 518].
    *   *With Lower Seat*: Set a gap on the weight stack to lower the handles to properly align with the lower seat setting, allowing the client to reach the stool comfortably [518].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs / Larger Frame**:
    *   Taller clients with longer torsos must sit further back in the seat to ensure they lean forward into the desired posture [412, 520].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Shoulder Joint Sensitivity / Deconditioned*: Lower seat settings may place the shoulders in excessive starting stretch (hyperextension) and the elbows in hyperflexion [520]. For these clients, err on a higher seat setting to protect the shoulder capsule [499, 520].
    *   *Joint-Friendly Pushing*: Encourage the pronated "capped" hand position using Fat Gripz to spread pressure out over a larger area of the palm [499, 519, 524].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Elbow Abduction & Forearm Path**: Confirm the upper arms are abducted 45–60 degrees, and verify that the elbows remain aligned directly behind the hands, tracking on the same path concentrically and eccentrically [498, 501, 519, 522, 528].
2.  **Back Pad Leverage**: Ensure the client leans forward slightly and is leveraging their mid-to-upper back firmly against the back pad to counteract the upward forces of the machine [500, 502, 521, 525].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround (elbows bent 90° or more) with chest up, shoulders retracted, and chin tucked [501, 524]. Establish a 3-5 second gradual load-up (cue: *"gradually build pressure down into the handles, leveraging off the back pad"*) to crack the stack [501, 504, 524, 529]. Maintain a controlled 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric tempo with zero momentum [505, 530].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change directions with no pause ("touch and go", "barely touch, barely start") as soon as the weights touch [503, 527].
    *   *Upper Turnaround (Fully Contracted)*: Because there is no mechanical end stop, the concentric movement terminates when three actions occur synergistically: (1) arms completely straighten, (2) shoulder blades protract, and (3) abdomen is fully contracted (PPT) [502, 514, 525]. Click only after observing this full contraction and brief pause [502, 526].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Drive your arms straight, and tighten down hard on the abdomen, keep breathing... [click], ease out, don't speed up"* [503, 527].

## 14. Abdominals

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Rectus Abdominis (Abs - Spinal Flexion, Posterior Pelvic Tilt) [11, 36].
*   **Synergists**: External Obliques (spinal flexion) [11, 36], Transverse Abdominis (hollowing) [11, 36], Pelvic Floor (pelvis stabilization) [11, 36], Rectus Femoris, Psoas (hip flexion) [11, 36].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height (Theoretical Alignment)**: Align the top of the client's iliac crest (hip point) with the top of the rear roller pad at the back of the seat [12, 14, 37].
*   **Femur Restraint (Knee Pad)**: Adjust the femur restraint so that the pads straddle the kneecaps evenly—the patella should be approximately centered in the opening, with the upper pad resting snugly above the knee on the femur and the lower pad resting snugly below the knee along the tibia [16, 38]. The pads should be snug enough to allow slight wiggle room but no gap [16, 38, 320].
*   **Foot Placement**: Place feet on the fixed footplate so that the knee angle is approximately congruent to the angle of the femur restraint [14, 15, 38].
*   **Movement Arm (Roller Pad)**: Unlock the movement arm (roller pad) and guide it down. The pad should rest in the armpits (or as close as comfortably possible) [16, 38, 39]. Have the client bring their elbows closer together and place their hands in front of their head in a "prayer" position [16, 39].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 5 or 6, set prior to client entry [11, 36, 366].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Depending on leg length, the heels may hang off the platform slightly to maintain congruency with the femur/tibia pads [366].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs / Top-Heavy Proportions**:
    *   Taller clients or those with a larger hip girdle may have more mass above the movement arm, making them "top-heavy" [13, 37]. This creates artificial leverage that does not come from target musculature, requiring more weight and introducing risk [13].
    *   For these top-heavy clients, the instructor must be highly attentive to their height and torso proportions, keeping the lower body locked extremely tight, and verifying the alignment prevents lower back hyperextension [13, 14].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Lumbar Flexion Contraindications*: Clients who do not tolerate lumbar flexion from an orthopedic standpoint (e.g. disc issues) should omit this exercise dynamically [21, 43].
    *   *Timed Static Contraction (TSC) Protocol*: For compromised lower backs or those who cannot tolerate dynamic flexion, set a heavy, unmovable weight. Position the movement arm at the approximate midpoint (biased slightly toward the contracted position) [22, 43]. Instruct a 90-second static contraction (30s at 50% effort, 30s at 75% effort, 30s at 100% effort) [22, 43].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Iliac Crest Alignment**: Verify that the top of the client's iliac crest is aligned with the machine's axis (represented by the rear roller pad) to ensure the axis of rotation of the machine aligns with the lumbar-pelvic axis [12, 14, 37].
2.  **Femur Restraint Lock**: Verify that the femur restraint straddles the knee joint snugly with zero gaps, preventing the client's legs from shifting, which would otherwise allow hip flexion to dominate the movement [16, 38, 320].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up (Contracted Start)**: This movement starts at the upper turnaround in the fully contracted ("crunched") position [17, 39]. The coach stands behind the client, manually assists the movement arm and the client down into full abdominal contraction (with the client curling down, rounding their back, and tightening their abdomen) [17, 368]. Perform an interpersonal load transfer, gradually releasing manual effort while cueing, *"Tighten those abdominal muscles hard, breathe freely... that... is... yours"* [17, 40, 369]. Ensure control before the client begins the eccentric phase [17, 378].
*   **Continuous Tension Cadence**: Command a slow 6-second eccentric (slowly uncurling the spine vertebrae-by-vertebrae from the bottom up) and a 6-second concentric (curling back down one vertebrae at a time from top to bottom) [18, 19, 40, 41, 10].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change directions with no pause ("touch and go", "seamlessly change directions") the instant the weights touch [18, 19, 40, 41].
    *   *Upper Turnaround (Fully Contracted)*: Focus on maximum abdominal contraction (drawing the ribcage down to the pelvis) rather than machine range of motion [20, 42]. Implement a brief 1-2 second pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [20, 42]. Click only after visual confirmation of full contraction and control [42].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Tighten down hard, trying to cramp your abdominals... keep rounding, keep still, keep breathing... [click], ease out"* [20, 21, 42].
    *   *UT Truncation Strategy*: For clients who instinctively rest at the top under heavy fatigue, the coach can place a selector pin in an upper hole of the weight stack (above the stack) to act as a hard end stop, truncating the terminal contracted range [23, 44].

## 15. Torso Rotation

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: External Obliques (spinal rotation), Internal Obliques (spinal rotation) [545, 552].
*   **Synergists**: Rotatores (spinal rotation) [545, 552], Hip Abductors (stabilizing knees via horizontal abduction) [545, 552], Rectus Abdominis (spinal flexion/posterior pelvic tilt support) [545, 552].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Position**: Set the seat to Position 1 or 5 (largest rotation) for most clients to maximize the rotational range [545, 553]. Ensure the seat is fully locked against the rotation stopper [546, 555].
*   **Arm Pads**: Set both arm pads to Position 1 (lowest and closest to the subject's torso) to maintain a tight arm path [554].
*   **Leg Pads**: Use thin pads as the default setting [553].
*   **Bracing & Wedging**: Have the client sit all the way back with their pelvis crowding the hip pads [556]. Secure the seat belt tightly [547, 556]. Have them drive their knees out into the leg pads with their feet positioned flat on the floor directly below their knees [547, 556, 559].
*   **Arm Wrapping**: Client reaches back and wraps both arms over the roller pads (forearms running along the pads, hands "capping" the ends with palms facing them) [547, 557].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: No gap is used on Torso Rotation as the movement starts at the lower turnaround in the stretched position [371, 555].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Use thick leg pads to push shorter legs closer together or to increase pelvis stability [553].
    *   If their feet do not touch the floor firmly, place yoga blocks under their feet to properly position the knees into the pads [556].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Turn the roller pad to Position 4 (highest/furthest setting) to accommodate a longer torso while keeping the pads snug against the chest [554].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Deconditioned or Limited Rotational Mobility*: Set the seat to the more conservative Positions 2 or 4 to reduce the starting rotational stretch [545, 553].
    *   *Osteoporosis / Osteopenia (Strict Contraindication)*: Dynamic spinal rotation is contraindicated for clients with osteoporosis or osteopenia due to fracture risk [546, 553, 565]. For these clients, use the Static Hold (SH) protocol (if medically cleared) [546, 550, 565]. Set seat to Position 2 or 4, align the carriage square to the client, perform an interpersonal handoff, and have them hold the isometric contraction for 20–60 seconds with zero dynamic movement [550, 551, 566].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Pelvic Wedging**: Verify that the pelvis is wedged completely back into the seat and hip pads, and that the knees remain driven outward into the leg pads, ensuring the lower body remains completely locked and silent throughout [547, 556].
2.  **Ribcage-Carriage Attachment**: Ensure the client's mid-back remains in contact with the back pad of the carriage in a posterior pelvic tilt, meaning the carriage turns strictly as an extension of the ribcage, not the arms [547, 549, 558, 560].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from lower turnaround (stretched position) with abdomen tight in a posterior pelvic tilt [371, 547]. Guide them through a gradual load-up to "attach" the carriage to the ribcage by first drawing the far-side roller into the body and then beginning to turn the ribcage [547, 548, 559]. Maintain a controlled 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric cadence [10].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change of direction with no pause ("touch and go", "drag through the bottom") as soon as weights touch [549, 564].
    *   *Upper Turnaround (Fully Contracted)*: Focus on maximum oblique contraction and a compact range of motion (not chasing excessive machine rotation) [548, 550, 561]. Implement a brief pause on the first two reps, and a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [548, 562]. Squeeze cue: *"Keep tight, keep breathing... lock it in place, no movement... [click], ease out... do not speed up"* [549, 563].
*   **Programming**: Because this exercise must be performed in both directions (rotating left and rotating right), limit the set to 4 to 7 reps in each direction [550, 568]. To prevent asymmetrical fatigue, avoid training to true concentric failure on the first side, rest sufficiently, and match the rep count on the second side [550, 568].
