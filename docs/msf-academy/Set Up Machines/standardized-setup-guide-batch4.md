# Standardized Baseline Setup Guide (Batch 4: Final 5 Machines)
### Onboarding Protocol for New Clients (Continuous-Tension, High-Intensity Training)

This guide completes the standardized baseline starting points and body-type variables for onboarding new clients on the final 5 machines of the Imagine Strength and studio lineup.

---

## 16. Lumbar Extension (Lower Back)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Erector Spinae (Spinal Erectors/Spinal Extensors), Multifidus, Quadratus Lumborum (QL's) [305, 316].
*   **Synergists & Stabilizers**: Gluteus Maximus, Latissimus Dorsi, Trapezius (Scapular Retraction/Adduction and downward Rotation) [305, 316].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Pad Height**: Set using both theoretical and practical alignment. *Theoretical*: Align the client's iliac crest with the top of the roller pad at the back of the seat [306, 317]. *Practical*: Position the seat height so that when the client sits upright, the back pad (movement arm) makes contact with a majority of the shoulder blade (scapular) area [318]. 
*   **Footplate Setting**: Locate the footplate so that when the client's feet are placed on the platform, the knee joint is positioned slightly higher than the hip joint, creating a slight downward angle of the femur toward the hips [307, 319, 360].
*   **Femur Restraint (Knee Pad)**: Position and tighten the femur restraint so that the pads straddle the knees evenly (the patella is centered in the opening, with the upper pad resting on the femur and the lower pad resting on the tibia) [307, 320, 361]. Pads must be snug with slight wiggle room and congruent knee angles [307, 320].
*   **Restraint Settings**: Fasten the seat belt tightly across the hip crease *after* the feet are on the footplate and the femur restraint is in place [307, 319, 361]. Tighten the femur restraint pads an additional turn once the client is brought into the starting position [308, 323].
*   **Weight Stack & Restraints**: Set the accessory stack first if needed, move the carriage all the way back, and place the selector pin in the main stack to lock the movement arm [306, 316]. Establish a conservative starting gap (default starting gap is 4) [359, 362].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Shorter clients will have a higher foot placement on the platform [361].
    *   Adjust foot placement vertically to maintain a knee angle congruent to the femur restraint pads [307, 319].
    *   *Discomfort Adjustment*: If the back pad contacts the cervical spine, the seat is too low and must be raised [318].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set seat height lower [306, 317].
    *   If the back pad falls below the shoulder blades, the seat is too high and must be lowered [318].
    *   Move the footplate further out to accommodate longer leg lengths [320].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Spinal Flexion Sensitivity / History of Back Issues*: Increase the starting gap to 5 or 6 to create a shallower lower turnaround, limiting flexion under load [322, 359]. Most of the benefit comes from the extension phase; avoid deep flexion [308, 322].
    *   *No-Flexion Protocol (Osteoporosis/Osteopenia)*: Avoid dynamic lumbar flexion entirely unless medically cleared [313, 330].
    *   *Timed Static Contraction (TSC) Alternative*: Pin a heavy weight to prevent movement, position the movement arm near the midpoint (biased slightly toward extension), and cue a 90-second static contraction (30s at 50% effort, 30s at 75% effort, 30s at 100% effort) [313, 331].
    *   *Knee height / Roller Pad Pressure*: If the client reports uncomfortable pressure on the lower back, check for waistline belts/pants [314, 333]. If the knee is too high relative to the hip, it pushes the femur back into the hip; let the footplate out slightly to lower the knee [314, 333].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Back Pad Contact**: Ensure the back pad lands squarely on the middle-to-upper scapular area. It must not ride up onto the neck (seat too low) or slip below the shoulder blades (seat too high) [306, 318].
2.  **Downward Femur Slope**: Confirm that the knees are positioned slightly higher than the hip joint [307, 319, 360]. If the knees are level or lower than the hips, the client will engage in excessive leg drive, converting the movement into a hip-extension pattern rather than isolated spinal extension [309, 325].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround (flexed position) [308, 321]. Prevent the client's instinct to throw the head back or perform a valsalva breath hold [323, 324]. Cue: *"Chin tucked, chest up, establish your breathing, and gradually begin to build pressure into the back pad by lifting the ribcage"* [309, 323, 362]. It should take 3–5 seconds to crack the stack [324, 328]. Maintain a slow, continuous 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric tempo [312, 329].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Stop concentric rotation when full comfortable range of motion is reached (or when the stopper abuts the frame) [310, 326]. Pause for 1–2 seconds on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [310, 326]. Click only after visual confirmation of full contraction and control [174, 326]. Squeeze cue: *"Chest up, back arched... lift your chest, keep arching, keep breathing"* [310, 325].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Continuous change of direction with no pause ("touch and go") [311, 328]. Cue before the turn: *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch, do not pause"* [311, 328].
    *   *Safety Notice*: **Never take Lumbar Extension to failure** [312, 330, 383]. Ensure every rep is completed with absolute, exacting control, and stop the set early if form begins to degrade [312, 330].

## 17. Cervical Extension (Neck)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Trapezius (Upper Back/Neck Extensors), Semispinalis Muscle Group, Splenius Capitis, Longissimus Muscle Group [161, 217].
*   **Synergists**: Erector Spinae, Multifidus, Quadratus Lumborum (QL's) [161, 217].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set seat height according to client height (taller = lower seat; shorter = higher seat) [162, 218]. This aligns the machine's axis of rotation vertically with the cervical spine, near C3–C5 [162, 218].
*   **Seat Position (Horizontal)**: Instruct the client to sit in the middle of the seat, **not fully back** [163, 218]. Sitting fully back places the cervical axis behind the machine's axis of rotation [163, 218].
*   **Practical Head Pad Alignment**: The back of the head must sit flush and securely in the open recess of the head pad (the most protruding portion of the skull sits in the recess) [165, 219]. 
*   **Back Pad Setting**: If the back pad is used, bring it forward to make light contact with the client's mid-back. It must **not** push them forward of the seat's midpoint [164, 218].
*   **Weight Stack & Restraints**: No starting gap is required; the lower turnaround is defined by comfortable flexion [167, 219]. The standard starting weight is 20 lbs (the lightest increment) [167, 219].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set the seat height higher to ensure the head pad recess meets the skull correctly [162, 218].
    *   If the head pad rides up over the top of the head during execution, the seat is too low and must be raised [166, 219].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set the seat height lower [162, 218].
    *   Ensure the client remains seated in the middle of the seat (not pushed back) [163, 218].
    *   If the back of the head sits above the opening recess, the seat is too high and must be lowered [166, 219].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Acute Neck Pain / Deconditioned*: Use a Static Hold (SH) or Timed Static Contraction (TSC) protocol to avoid rotational movements [178, 223].
    *   *Static Protocol Setup*: Position the movement arm just beyond neutral (chin slightly elevated). Instruct the client to press back and down statically for 30–45 seconds at a moderate, pain-free effort [178, 223, 384].
    *   *Range of Motion Restrictions*: If the client has limited neck mobility, restrict dynamic flexion/extension to a comfortable, pain-free range, stopping well short of mechanical boundaries [172, 222].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Flush Head Pad Contact**: Verify that the most protruding portion of the skull is seated perfectly in the open recess of the head pad [165, 219]. Assess this alignment by standing to the **side** of the client [169, 220]. If any sliding or shifting of the skull occurs during execution, stop and adjust [166, 219].
2.  **Middle-Seat Anchoring**: Confirm the client is seated in the middle of the pad. If they have slid all the way back, their neck axis is out of horizontal alignment with the machine's axis [163, 218].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up (Critical Interpersonal Transfer)**: Pull the head pad back before the client sits [163, 218]. Cue: *"Sit tall... chin up... scoot forward"* [169, 220]. With hands on the front handles and elbows tucked in to clear the moving frame, bring the pad to the head [168, 220, 377]. Instruct the client to increase their backward force to receive the load [170, 378]. Once stable with zero movement, hand off with the cue: *"That is yours"* [170, 220]. If the arm moves during transfer, regain control and cue them to push harder [170, 378].
*   **Concentric Phase**: Instruct the client to perform a rotary movement, not a linear push [177, 222]. Cue them to draw a "rainbow" or "arch" with the top of their head [175, 177, 221, 380]. Cue: *"Lift your head and chin while arching your back and lifting your chest... head to butt, butt to head"* [173, 221, 381, 382].
*   **Eccentric Phase**: Cue: *"Let the head come forward... maintain flush contact with the pad"* [170, 220, 379]. The ribcage may need to drop slightly as the head moves forward to keep flush contact against the head pad [171, 220].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Touch and go seamlessly with no pause at the bottom [172, 221, 381]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch, do not pause"* [172, 221, 383].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Pause briefly (1–2s on first two reps, 2–3s squeeze from third rep onward) [174, 221, 382]. Click only after movement stops at maximal safe extension [174, 221].
    *   *Safety Notice*: **NEVER take Cervical Extension to failure** [175, 222, 383]. Stop the set early (typically ≤8 reps) and progress very conservatively [175, 179, 223].

## 18. Abduction (Hips/Glutes)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Gluteus Medius (Hip horizontal abduction) [1, 24].
*   **Synergists**: Gluteus Minimus, Gluteus Maximus (lateral fibers), Piriformis [1, 24].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back Position**: Standardize at Position 6 or 7 to maintain an upright posture [1, 24].
*   **Thigh Pad Width**: Establish the starting position by adjusting the width of the thigh pads (which determines the starting stretch and eliminates the need for a weight stack gap) [2, 25]. Thigh pads should be set as "snug" as possible, even slightly compressing soft tissue ("crowding") to maximize range of motion [2, 25].
*   **Seat Belt**: Secure the seat belt tightly across the waist/hip crease (ensuring it does not pull down on or restrict the femurs) [3, 27, 355].
*   **Hand Handles**: Instruct the client to place their hands on the side handles for balance, not for anchoring or pulling down [3, 27].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: No gap is required; the starting position is set by the tight starting width of the thigh pads [2, 25].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set the thigh pads to narrower starting positions to accommodate a smaller pelvis [2, 26].
    *   If shorter legs cause the feet to hover, instruct the client to rest their feet back on the toes of their shoes to maintain stability [117, 128].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set the thigh pads to wider starting positions [2, 26].
    *   Ensure the hips are pushed fully back to stabilize the pelvis [3, 27].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Hip Replacement / Pathologies*: Clients with hip replacements or acute hip pathologies may not tolerate dynamic abduction [8, 34].
    *   *TSC Protocol Alternative*: Pin a weight too heavy to move, set the thigh pads near the midpoint of the range, and cue a 90-second static contraction (30s at 50% effort, 30s at 75% effort, 30s at 100% effort) [8, 9, 34].
    *   *History of Hip Discomfort*: Use a slightly wider, less compressed starting thigh pad setting to reduce the initial stretch [2, 25, 26].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Anterior Pelvic Tilt (Arch Back)**: Verify the client sits with a "chest up, butt back, back arched" posture [3, 27]. This anterior pelvic tilt is critical for optimal alignment and motor unit recruitment of the gluteus medius [3, 27].
2.  **Thigh Pad Congruency**: Confirm that the thigh pads sit flush against the client's knees/thighs [33, 355]. Note that while driving out through the knees is simpler to coach, the actual lever being moved is the femur [8, 34].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround (pads snug) [3, 27]. Cue: *"Lift your chest, arch your back, hands on handles, and gradually apply outward pressure through the knees"* [3, 27, 355]. Once the stack cracks, command a smooth 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric tempo [10, 35].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround (Concentric Phase)*: Drive knees apart as far as comfortably possible while maintaining the chest-up, arched-back posture [4, 28]. Pause briefly for 1–2 seconds on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [4, 28, 30]. Click at the exact moment contraction stops [4, 28]. Squeeze cue: *"Lift your chest, arch your back, keep driving out through your knees as if the movement continues... keep breathing, keep still"* [6, 30].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Continuous change of direction with no pause ("touch and go") [5, 29]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch, touch and go"* [29, 30].
    *   *Pinch Points Warning*: Always pull the movement arms apart by grabbing the *ends* of the padding, and keep client hands clear of the rocking/tilting thigh pads during entry and exit [7, 33].

## 19. Adduction (Inner Thighs)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Adductor Brevis, Adductor Longus, Adductor Magnus, Pectineus, Gracilis (Inner Thigh) [92, 102].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Back Position**: Standardize at Position 6 or 7 [92, 103].
*   **Thigh Pad Settings / Start Position**: Establish a conservative starting range of motion by setting a custom starting gap on the weight stack for every client to ensure a tolerable level of stretch [93, 103, 111].
*   **Entry / Exit Procedure (Critical)**: Clear the weight stack completely [93, 103]. Have the client sit, then bring the movement arms together [93, 104]. Cue the client to lift their left leg into the pad, then their right leg, pushing the arms closer to assist [93, 104]. Slowly allow the movement arms to drift apart (keeping your leg/hand against the mechanism to prevent sudden drops) until the client indicates a gentle stretch on the inner thigh [93, 105]. Pin the stack at that gap [93, 105].
*   **Posture**: Sit back, butt back, neutral head, feet and ankles relaxed, and hands on handles for balance only (no anchoring) [94, 106].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Customized starting gap based on a gentle, comfortable stretch [93, 105, 111].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set a more conservative range of motion (narrower gap) to avoid over-stretching short muscle fibers [111].
    *   Ensure the upper body remains relaxed and they do not scoot forward excessively [94, 106].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Taller clients may tolerate a slightly wider gap (more stretch), but onboarding must remain highly conservative [111].
    *   Ensure hips remain firmly back against the seat back [94, 106].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Hip Replacement / Pathologies*: Omit dynamic training entirely or utilize a Timed Static Contraction (TSC) protocol [99, 112].
    *   *TSC Protocol Setup*: Set movement arms at the midpoint of the range, select a weight too heavy to lift, and cue inward pressing: 30s @ 50% effort, 30s @ 75% effort, 30s @ 100% effort [99, 100, 112].
    *   *Over-stretching Cautions*: Always err on a conservative stretch initially [111]. Excessive stretching under load in the adduction pattern carries high injury risk with diminishing hypertrophy returns [111].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Trainer Control of Entry/Exit**: The instructor must entirely control the movement arms during leg entry and exit, preventing any sudden movements [105, 111]. Verify knees are centered in the pads, driving the movement through the knees/femurs rather than the lower legs [98, 112].
2.  **Upper Body Relaxation**: Ensure the chest, shoulders, and jaw are completely relaxed, and hands rest lightly on handles for balance, rather than pulling up to anchor [94, 106].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround starting gap [94, 106, 350]. Cue: *"Establish your breathing, hands resting lightly on handles, and gradually apply inward pressure, driving your knees together"* [94, 106, 350]. Maintain a strict 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric tempo [100, 113].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround (Concentric Phase)*: Drive knees together smoothly until the pads touch [95, 107]. Ensure the effort comes solely through the knees (femurs), keeping feet and ankles completely relaxed [351, 352]. Pause for 1–2 seconds on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [95, 108]. Click at the exact moment handles converge with zero fluttering [107, 351]. Squeeze cue: *"Continue to drive your knees together... keep breathing, lock it in place, [click], ease out... do not speed up"* [96, 108].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Touch and go seamlessly with no pause at the bottom [96, 108]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch, pass through without pausing"* [95, 107].

## 20. Lateral Raise (Shoulders)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Middle Deltoid (Sides of the shoulders) [420].
*   **Synergists & Stabilizers**: Anterior Deltoid, Triceps, Pectoralis Major, Rectus Abdominis, Trapezius (Scapular rotation and stabilizer) [420].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Adjusted based on client height so that the upper arm aligns horizontally with the movement arm pads [420].
*   **Back Pad Position**: Set to the "up" position (^ for most) [420].
*   **Handle Settings**: Default to Position 1 or Position 2 on the handles, ensuring that **both** handles are pinned in the exact same slot [420].
*   **Foot Support**: Provide a standard footstool so that the feet are firmly supported, which assists in keeping a stable posture [420, 422].
*   **Arm Placement**: Open the handle levers to allow seating [420]. Have the client sit all the way back, close the handles, and place their arms inside the pads with their hands resting open on the handles [420].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: No gap is used by default [419, 420].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Raise the seat height to align the shoulders with the pad axes [420].
    *   Use a footstool to prevent the legs from dangling [420, 422].
    *   Use Handle Position 1 to accommodate shorter forearm lengths [420].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Lower the seat height [420].
    *   Use Handle Position 2 or 3 to accommodate longer arm spans [420].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Shoulder/Neck Discomfort (Trapezius Dominance)*: If the client experiences neck strain or has a tendency to shrug excessively, shorten the range of motion, stopping the concentric sweep slightly below parallel [421, 422]. A shorter ROM is still highly effective and keeps tension focused on the deltoids [421].
    *   *Rotator Cuff Issues*: If dynamic abduction is painful, implement a midpoint Timed Static Contraction (TSC) or Static Hold [243, 244].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Abdominal Bracing & Back Contact**: Verify the client's mid-back is rounded and firmly in contact with the back pad [420]. The client must maintain a tight posterior pelvic tilt (abdominal wall contracted, ribcage drawn down) [420, 422]. If the client arches their back or lifts their chest, they will shrug, transferring the load from the deltoids to the upper trapezius and neck [420, 422].
2.  **Symmetrical Handle Pinning**: Double-check that both handles are locked in the identical position (e.g., Position 1 on both sides) to prevent asymmetrical loading [420].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround (resting position) [420]. Cue: *"Tighten down your midsection so the middle of your back rounds into the back pad. Draw your ribcage down, keep your head still, breathe freely, and begin to gradually apply pressure OUT through the elbow pads"* [420]. Crack the stack over 3–5 seconds and maintain a controlled 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric tempo [150, 420].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Drive outward through the elbows until the upper arms are parallel to the floor (or slightly below depending on shoulder mobility) [421]. Pause briefly for 1–2 seconds on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [423]. Click only after movement stops and control is demonstrated [422]. Cue: *"Keep breathing, head still, drive OUT through the elbows... keep still, [click], ease out... do not speed up"* [422].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless change of direction with no pause ("touch and go") [424]. Cue: *"Be ready to seamlessly change direction as soon as those weights touch, do not pause at the bottom"* [424].
