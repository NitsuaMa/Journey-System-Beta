# Standardized Baseline Setup Guide (Batch 2: Machines 6-10)
### Onboarding Protocol for New Clients (Continuous-Tension, High-Intensity Training)

This guide provides the standardized baseline starting points and body-type variables for onboarding new clients on the next five machines of the Imagine Strength and studio lineup.

---

## 6. Pulldown (Torso Arm)

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Latissimus Dorsi (Lats - shoulder extension, adduction) [460, 469].
*   **Synergists & Secondaries**: Teres Major, Posterior Deltoid, Biceps Brachii, Brachioradialis (elbow flexion, forearm supination) [460, 469].
*   **Stabilizers & Scapular Adjusters**: Rhomboids (scapular downward rotation), Lower Trapezius (scapular depression), Pectoralis Major, Triceps Brachii, Erector Spinae, Rectus Abdominis (abdominal wall/PPT support), Wrist Flexors (grip support) [460, 461, 469, 470].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set using seat positions 5–9 [461, 472]. Avoid positions 1–4, which are too high for standard clients [461, 470]. The seat height should allow the subject's arm to almost but not quite fully straighten at the lower turnaround (weights touching), maintaining a slight elbow bend [462].
*   **Back Pad Position**: Set to position 2 or 3 for most clients [461, 471]. This ensures the vertical path of the handles is positioned out in front of the subject (behind the handle path by at least the length of the upper arm) [471].
*   **Restraint Settings**: Securely fasten and tighten the seat belt across the hip crease to prevent the client's body from being pulled upward by the load [463, 473].
*   **Grip & Handle Settings**: Underhand supinated grip with handles pointing in toward each other and slightly forward (one notch toward neutral) [461, 471]. Hands must be positioned shoulder-width or slightly narrower to keep a narrow, lat-leveraging path [462, 473].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting gap of 2 [388]. Set the gap by bringing handles down to the client's reach while maintaining a slight elbow bend [462, 472].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set the seat closer to the floor (positions 5–9) to make entry/exit safer and ensure they can reach the handles [461, 472].
    *   Increase the weight stack gap to bring the handles further down to meet the client's reach [462, 472].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a higher seat position [462].
    *   For very large or wide frames, set the back pad further back to position 1 [461, 471].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Elbow Valgus / Hand/Wrist/Shoulder Issues*: Switch to the **Torso Arm** variation [466, 467, 481]. This uses a neutral grip (handles pointing forward, parallel to each other) and operates in the frontal plane, which alters the lat bias (biasing the iliac/lower division rather than the thoracic/lumbar divisions) and is much friendlier on compromised joints [466, 467, 479].
    *   *Starting extension weakness / joint pain*: Increase the weight stack gap to limit elbow extension, creating a shallower lower turnaround [481].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Forearm Path & Grip Position**: Confirm that the vertical path of the handles remains out in front of the subject by at least the length of the upper arm, keeping the forearm aligned with the movement path [461, 471].
2.  **Scapulohumeral Rhythm**: Allow the shoulder blades to move naturally during the eccentric phase. Do not over-correct or restrict the scapulae unless excessive, unnatural shrugging occurs [467, 480].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up**: This movement requires an instructor-assisted handoff near the contracted position (handles pulled down to about eye level, where the client is in a biomechanically strong position) [388, 389, 474]. The coach assists the client in pulling the handles down, secures a stable stance, and transfers the load using the cue: *"That is yours"* once control is established [388, 389, 463, 474].
*   **Continuous Tension Cadence**: Cue a controlled, smooth 6-second eccentric (allowing the movement arm to rise) and a 6-second concentric (elbows driving down) [468].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless "touch and go" change of direction with no pause at the top of the range [389, 464, 475]. Weights lightly touch, and the client immediately begins pulling down [464, 475].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: The concentric phase terminates when the handles are just below chin level (humerus aligned with the side of the torso) [476]. Pause briefly (1–2 seconds) on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [390, 465]. Click only after visual confirmation of full range of motion and control [465, 477].
    *   *Turnaround Cue*: *"Drive elbows into the sides of your ribcage... there... keep still, breathe freely, [click], ease out"* [465, 478].

## 7. Overhead Press

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Anterior Deltoid, Middle Deltoid, Triceps Brachii (elbow extension) [430, 437].
*   **Synergists**: Pectoralis Major (assists in shoulder flexion), Rectus Abdominis (torso/pelvic stability via PPT), Subscapularis, Posterior Deltoid, Trapezius (scapular stabilizer) [430, 437].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set so that when the client sits with arms hanging naturally at their sides and bends only at the elbow, the hands align directly with the ends of the handles [431, 438].
*   **Grip & Hand Position**: Place hands near the bottom/ends of the handles with palms facing inward (neutral grip) [431, 441]. Ensure the forearm remains perpendicular to the downward-angled handle to align with the arcing movement path [431, 439].
*   **Restraints & Foot Anchor**: A standard footstool is mandatory for all clients to support the legs, prevent dangling, and stabilize the pelvis [431, 442].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 2 [431, 438].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set a higher seat height [435].
    *   Provide a stool for entry/exit if needed, and assist them into position [431, 438].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a lower seat height [435, 448].
    *   Increase the weight stack gap beyond the standard of 2 to maintain favorable, safe starting joint angles [448].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Compromised Shoulder Joint*: Lower the seat height and/or increase the weight stack gap to shorten the range of motion in the bottom (deep stretch) portion of the movement [431, 439, 448].
    *   *Upper-Range Shoulder Discomfort*: If the client experiences pain in the upper half of the movement, shorten the ROM and use a selector pin to establish a hard end stop near the midpoint of the concentric phase [435, 448].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Scapular Plane Elbow Alignment**: Confirm the upper arms track in the scapular plane (angled 30°–40° anterior to the frontal plane; elbows pointed slightly forward, not flared out to the sides) [432, 441].
2.  **Posterior Pelvic Tilt (PPT)**: Visually verify that the client maintains an active posterior pelvic tilt with a contracted abdomen and a rounded middle back into the back pad to protect the lumbar spine [432, 440].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround (the starting position) [433, 442]. Cue the client to tuck the chin slightly, gaze downward, and gradually apply pressure through the heels of the palms over 3–5 seconds to "crack the stack" [432, 433, 443]. Maintain a smooth, continuous 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric pace [436].
*   **Continuous Change of Direction**: As a compound pushing movement, there is **no pause or squeeze** at either turnaround [434, 435, 445].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: The arms should completely straighten, allowing some natural scapular elevation [434, 444]. Click at the exact moment of full extension/end of ROM [434, 445]. Immediately cue *"ease out, do not speed up"* to begin the eccentric smoothly [434, 445].
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly change direction without pausing. Barely let the plates touch [411, 434, 445]. Cue: *"Barely touch, smoothly and continuously change directions"* [411, 434].

## 8. Chest Flye

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Pectoralis Major (chest/pecs - shoulder horizontal adduction) [138, 180].
*   **Synergists**: Anterior Deltoid (shoulder horizontal adduction) [138, 180].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set so that when seated upright, the client's upper arm (humerus) slopes down slightly from the shoulder to the elbow, or is at least level with the floor in the starting position [138, 181]. The humeral head should be level with or slightly higher than the elbow when hands are on the handles [139, 181].
*   **Back Pad Depth**: Set the back pad so that when sitting upright with arms out to the sides and hands on the handles, the elbow lands beyond the roller pad and remains in contact with it throughout the full range of motion [139, 182].
*   **Restraints & Foot Anchor**: Provide a standard footstool for all clients to support the feet and maintain a stable chest-up posture [140, 184].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 1 or 2 to create a conservative starting stretch [140, 183].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set the back pad closer to the roller pads [139, 182].
    *   *Alternate Hand Position*: If the client has short arms and cannot reach the handles without forcing the humerus into an upward angle, have them wrap their hands around the top of the roller pads instead of using the handles [145, 190].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set the back pad further back, away from the roller pads [139, 182].
    *   Set a lower seat height [138, 180].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Compromised Shoulder Joint*: Increase the weight stack gap to establish a more conservative starting point, keeping the elbows from traveling too far behind the midline of the torso (frontal plane) [140, 183].
    *   *Wrist/Hand/Elbow Limitations*: This machine is an excellent chest-focused option for clients who cannot tolerate heavy grip or wrist/elbow loading, as the force is directed through the elbows against the pads [145, 191].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Elbow-Pad Contact**: Verify that the elbows remain in contact with and positioned beyond the roller pads throughout the entire range of motion [139, 182].
2.  **Ribcage Elevation**: Ensure the client maintains a strict chest-up posture with the ribcage lifted and locked, preventing them from rounding their shoulders forward to force the pads together [141, 143, 187].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start from the lower turnaround (elbows slightly behind the midline of the torso) [140, 183]. Cue a gradual load-up by driving forward through the elbows [141, 184]. Maintain a slow, continuous 6-second concentric and 6-second eccentric pace [146].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless "touch and go" change of direction with no pause at the bottom [142, 186]. Cue: *"Barely touch, barely start"* or *"Seamlessly change direction as the weights touch"* [142, 186].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Smoothly drive the elbows together until the roller pads make contact [141, 185]. Pause briefly (1–2 seconds) on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [141, 142, 185, 187]. Click only after confirming full ROM and control [141, 185].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Keep your chest up and gently bring those pads together... now keep breathing and continue to drive through your elbows, attempting to further dent those pads... keep still... [click], ease out... don’t speed up"* [143, 187].

## 9. Biceps Curl

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Biceps Brachii (elbow flexion) [114, 123].
*   **Synergists**: Wrist Flexors (wrist stabilizers/forearm grip) [114, 123].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set so that when sitting upright with the chest firmly against the chest pad, the entire length of the upper arm (humerus) lays flush along the arm pad [115, 125, 126]. The elbow crease points toward the ceiling, requiring slight external rotation of the humeri [116, 125].
*   **Grip & Hand Position**: Grip the handles with palms facing up, positioned equidistant from the center [116, 127]. Ensure the wrists remain flat and straight throughout the movement [116, 117, 127].
*   **Restraints & Feet**: Feet must be flat on the floor [117, 128].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized starting weight stack gap of 1 or 2 to establish a safe lower turnaround and protect the elbow joint under load at full extension [114, 123, 135].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set a higher seat height [115, 124].
    *   Rest feet back on the toes of the shoes if they cannot touch the floor flat [117, 128].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a lower seat height [115, 124].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Elbow Vulnerability / Valgus Elbow*: Maintain a slightly larger gap to prevent extreme elbow extension at the bottom [120, 135]. Ensure some degree of bend remains in the elbow at the lower turnaround [121, 135].
    *   *Joint Issues / Tennis or Golfer's Elbow*: Substitute with an isometric protocol, such as a Timed Static Contraction (TSC), pinning an immovable load at a comfortable midpoint and building static effort (50%, 75%, 100%) for 90 seconds [121, 136].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Arm Pad Flushness**: Check that the humerus lies completely flush along the pad [126]. If the seat is too high, the upper arm will hover; if too low, the elbow won't make contact, causing uncomfortable pressure on the triceps at the pad's bend [115, 126].
2.  **Flat Wrists**: Verify that wrists remain flat and straight. If the client flexes the wrists or lifts their elbows off the pad, they are outsourcing the effort to the forearms and shoulders [122, 137].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: The instructor removes belt slack by lifting the handle from the resting position to the starting lower turnaround [116, 127]. Instruct the client: *"The exercise will begin from this position"* [116, 128]. Gradually load up by driving the elbows down into the pad and pulling the handles up [117, 128]. Maintain a smooth 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric tempo [122, 136].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless "touch and go" change of direction with no pause at the bottom [119, 131]. Monitor closely to ensure the transition is controlled and stable [119, 132].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Flex the elbows tightly to the maximum contraction point without lifting the arms off the pad [118, 129, 137]. Pause briefly (1–2 seconds) on reps 1–2, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from rep 3 onward [118, 130]. Click only after confirming full contraction and control [120, 134].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Continue to tighten your elbow joint as hard as you can... keep breathing, keep still, [click], ease out, don't speed up"* [130] or *"keeping your head still, clamp your elbow joints tight... there, lock it in place, [click], ease out"* [130].

## 10. Triceps Extension

### Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Triceps Brachii (elbow extension) [569].
*   **Synergists**: Rectus Abdominis (abdominal stabilizer, critical on machines without a back pad) [569].

### The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set so that the entire length of the upper arm (humerus) lays flush along the arm pad with the chest resting upright against the chest pad [570, 571].
*   **Back Pad Position**: Bring the back pad forward to make contact with the client's upper back, holding them securely against the chest pad to oppose the reactionary forces of the movement arm [572, 574].
*   **Hand Position**: Place the hands on the movement arm pads with palms facing each other (bladed hands or clenched fists) [425, 575].
*   **Restraints & Stability**: For machines without a back pad, place feet under the foot pads and cinch the seat belt tightly across the lap to prevent vertical lift [574].
*   **Weight Stack Gap**: Typically, no gap is used (Gap 0) as the starting position is established from full elbow flexion [424, 582].

### Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   Set a higher seat height [569].
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   Set a lower seat height [569].
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Movement Arm Proximity to Face / Initial Weakness*: If the movement arm at rest sits too close to the subject's face, or if the client is too weak to initiate the load from deep elbow flexion, set a gap of 1 to 3 to shorten the starting flexion range [572, 573, 582].
    *   *Elbow Pain / Tendinitis*: Switch to an isometric protocol, such as a Static Hold or Timed Static Contraction (TSC), using a comfortable midpoint to safely build tension [583].

### Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Arm Pad Flushness & Elbow Control**: Verify that the entire humerus remains completely flush against the arm pad. Correct immediately if the client lifts their elbows off the pad to push the movement arm further [571, 584].
2.  **Head Position & Clearance**: Confirm that the movement arm clears the client's head/face on the lower turnaround, and that the client does not duck or drop their head forward during the concentric phase [428, 429].

### Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Load-Up / Cadence**: Start directly from the lower turnaround (deep elbow flexion) [425, 572, 575]. Tighten the abdomen, relax the face, and gradually apply pressure by driving the edge of the hands into the pads and pushing away [425, 575]. Maintain a smooth 6-second concentric, 6-second eccentric pace [421, 583].
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamless "touch and go" change of direction as soon as the weights touch, without pausing [428, 577]. Cue: *"Seamlessly change directions"* [424].
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Fully straighten the elbows as much as comfortably possible [426, 576, 578]. Pause briefly (1–2 seconds) on the first two reps, and implement a 2–3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward [426, 427, 576, 577]. Click only after visual confirmation that movement has ceased and maximum extension is achieved [579].
    *   *Squeeze Cue*: *"Continue to drive the edge of your hands into the pad as you straighten your elbows as hard as you can... keep breathing, keep still, [click], ease out, don't speed up"* [578] or *"maintain the contact of your upper arm as you drive your elbows straight... lock it in place, [click], ease out"* [578, 580].
