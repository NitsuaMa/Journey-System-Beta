LC – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles:
Hamstrings – Knee Flexion
Gastrocnemius (Calf) – Knee Flexion
Synergists:
Sartorius – Assists in Knee Flexion
Gracilis – Assists in Knee Flexion
Wrist Flexors – Gripping/Holding – anchoring into seat with arms
Considerations for Setup:
Set an initial gap of 1 or 2 on the weight stack as an introduction to the exercise (this will be determined by the Studio Leader and MSF Corporate).  A gap of 3 or more may be required for knee injuries/issues
Move seat to rearmost position to allow maximum surface area
Subject sits with femurs cantilevered off front edge of seat and lower legs hanging freely
Patellas must not be under the roller pad; they should approximate or “crowd” the pad, with the top of tibia under the pad
Subject leans slightly forward, seat back is brought forward to just contact the lower back without pushing knees ahead of axis
Verify knees are aligned with axis of rotation
Subject exits the machine to allow for weight selection and proper loading
Use assist bar and trainer’s right knee to pull the movement arm down to mid-range, allowing subject to sweep legs in from the side
Lower the weight gently to establish starting position with clients legs extended and between pads
ALTERNATE LOADING: If the weight is too heavy to assist manually, use only the accessory stack initially, then select main stack weight after the subject is loaded
Adjust the calf pad to contact the lowest part of the back of the leg just above the achilles tendon so as not to contact the shoe
Posture/Get Set:
Chest up, lay back against seat with head neutral and eyes forward
Hands firmly gripping handles, pulling up for leverage
Feet and ankles relaxed during setup and initial repetitions
Secure seat belt across hip crease and pull tight
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds:
Begin from lower turn with feet/ankles relaxed and pulling up on handles
Gradually build pressure by bending knees into roller pad to create force couple with the lower leg
Instructor gives cue to initiate load up; observe movement carefully
First concentric ends with a brief pause at maximal knee flexion; cue with “mmm…hmm” or “just like that” if properly controlled then [click]
After first concentric, cue “ease out and do not speed up”
Establish lower turnaround expectations: “touch and go,” “barely touch, barely start,” or “seamless change of direction”
Keep hips down by anchoring with hands if necessary
From third rep on, dorsiflex ankles to better leverage calves after the lower turn and during concentric
Cue: “keep the ankles relaxed during the turn… now pull the feet up to the knees”
At upper turn, cue: “keep ankles flexed, keep movement arm still, keep breathing, [click], relax ankles, THEN ease out”
Ensure no movement occurs during ankle relaxation
Maintain consistent pace with brief 1-2 second pauses on first two reps, and 2–3 second “squeeze” from third rep on
Teach HOW to squeeze with detailed cues such as: “pulling up on the handles, drive your knees into the pad, heels toward rear end, keep breathing, [click], relax ankles, ease out”
Maintain posture, neutral head, consistent arm leverage, and proper contact on roller pads
Movement should only occur at the knee joint; arms may engage more as leverage increases
Record only full reps; perform partial reps only if experienced and able to control beyond concentric failure
For new clients or those in discomfort, the set may end before loss of range of motion (i.e. concentric failure)
Encourage maintaining pace with increasing effort as fatigue builds
Educate that mechanical tension increases as voluntary effort overcomes increasing fatigue
NOTES:
Describe gradual pressure during load-up; use examples such as 20–40–60 lbs of pressure or “just enough to crack the stack”
Dorsiflexion is introduced after the first two reps to engage the gastrocnemius for added strength and deeper contraction
Ankles are relaxed during eccentric to isolate hamstrings
Toggling between dorsiflexion and relaxation improves control and enhances muscle awareness
Adjust intensity based on the client’s conditioning level; less intensity needed for beginners
Teach intensity progression through quality control and calm focus
Expect and address common discomforts or fears of cramps as often they are unfamiliar sensations, may not be true cramping
If full knee flexion is not achievable, redefine full range of motion for that client and document accordingly (e.g. limited ROM)
Consider using a hard end stop with the extra selector pin at the subject’s contracted position to improve consistency and provide a point to pull into
Document pin placement using the number of empty holes between the top of the stack and the selector pin (i.e. gap for the upper turn)
Full contraction is not necessary for benefit but may help with establishing a neuromuscular connection
Continuous Tension protocol will likely start with lighter weights and higher reps
Lighter loads may lead to discomfort before stimulus; strive for faster tempo over time (6 sec concentric, 6 sec eccentric) with heavier loads once control is established
Adjust progression based on the client’s demonstrated control and tolerance.  Progress slowly for those that warrant it
