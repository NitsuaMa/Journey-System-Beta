CR – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Rhomboids – Scapular Retraction/Adduction and Downward Rotation
Trapezius – Scapular Retraction/Adduction and Downward Rotation
Latissimus Dorsi – Shoulder Extension, Shoulder Adduction
Posterior Deltoid – Shoulder Horizontal Abduction, Shoulder Extension
Teres Major – Shoulder Extension
Biceps – Elbow Flexion, Forearm Supination
Brachioradialis – Elbow Flexion, Forearm Pronation, Supination
Synergists
Erector Spinae – Spinal Extension, Anterior Pelvic Tilt (chest-up posture)
Wrist Flexors – Wrist Flexion (grip support)
Considerations for Setup
Set chest pad height based on client’s torso length and comfort; choose “up” or “down” position and note on chart
Adjust seat so handles are just within fingertip reach with arms fully extended and shoulders rounded forward slightly
Use standard gap of 2 on the weight stack unless otherwise determined with help of MSF Corporate.  Match chest pad location up to the handles
Confirm movement arm mechanism will clear top of client’s head
Move chest pad back and increase weight stack gap if movement arm is too close
Use middle handle width setting by default (“M” on chart); choose narrow or wide settings based on body size or target emphasis
Align handle settings with training intention (narrower for lats, wider for upper back)—random variation compromises outcome
Posture / Get Set
Chest up, hips back, slight forward lean into chest pad
Ensure hips are not tucked under chest pad as this encourages leaning back
Client and trainer pull handles together just beyond midpoint for handoff
Ensure client can contribute to initial handle retrieval—leverage is poor for trainer alone
Trainer should adopt wide stance and use both bodyweight and correct grip for assist
Use one hand on bottom of arm and one on pronated handle when assisting
Cue client during load transfer with, “That is yours,” or similar language
Delay handoff if client cannot yet stabilize—cue “pull harder” or “increase your force”
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Begin each set near the contracted (upper turnaround) position with trainer assist.  Actual handoff should occur just beyond midpoint
Reinforce scapular retraction and upright posture during handoff to establish target muscle awareness
After controlled handoff, cue subject to “slowly begin to let the arms straighten”
Once arms are extended, cue to “continuously” or “immediately” begin the concentric phase
Encourage seamless turnarounds—avoid any pauses or hesitation at LT
Weights may or may not touch at the bottom; allow if it reinforces consistent ROM or protects valgus elbow
Upper turnaround is defined by full contraction: elbows pass torso or align at torso side (depending on row variation)
Discourage excessive backward pulling that shifts focus to non-target tissues
Click only after full ROM and controlled pause have been achieved
Be aware that clients may wait for the click to determine when to stop, but it must be earned by contraction and control
Use specific verbal cues: “Draw the shoulders back,” “Pull with shoulder blades,” “Drive your elbows back,” (upper back-biased) or “Pull elbows into your sides” (lat-biased)
Maintain chest-up posture, neutral head, and consistent arm path on both concentric and eccentric
Elbows must follow identical paths on both phases to ensure precise loading
Intensity
End set at point of concentric failure with perfect form (or sooner for new/intensity adverse clients)
Record only complete repetitions on chart
Consider use of partial reps beyond failure if client is experienced and capable
Evaluate tolerance and readiness before applying extended set techniques
New clients may achieve stimulus from full reps only—progress intensity gradually
Reinforce that effort must increase to maintain constant pace as fatigue rises
Educate clients on the concept of increasing effort as a response to fatigue
Stimulative mechanical tension is created by matching or exceeding effort demands
Notes
Ensure movement arm clears the client’s head during entry, exit, and execution
Use neutral grip handles as default—they are generally the most shoulder-friendly
Set up so client can assist during entry and can release handles independently after set
Narrow arm paths bias the lats; wider arm paths bias upper back (trapezius, rhomboids, rear delts)
Scapular motion should increase with upper back emphasis—encourage retraction and protraction
Be aware of valgus elbow conditions—adjust ROM or switch to pronated handles if discomfort occurs
Pronated grip requires wider arm path and further elbow drive for upper back emphasis
Adjust chest pad height based on spine length or unique needs (e.g., large abdomen or sternum sensitivity)
In early stages of Continuous Tension, expect higher reps due to lighter loads
Avoid excessively long time under tension if discomfort outweighs stimulus
Prioritize perfect form and gradually increase resistance and possibly the pace (6-second concentric/6-second eccentric, ≤10 reps/set)
Progress intensity only after demonstrating control and understanding of the movement
