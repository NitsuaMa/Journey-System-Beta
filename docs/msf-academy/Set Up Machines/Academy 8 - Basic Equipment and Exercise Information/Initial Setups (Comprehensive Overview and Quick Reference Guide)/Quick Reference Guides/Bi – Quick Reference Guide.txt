Biceps Curl – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles:
Biceps Brachii (Biceps) – Elbow flexion
Synergists:
Wrist Flexors (Forearms) – Wrist stabilizer
Considerations for Setup:
Seat height is the primary setup consideration
A gap (usually 1–2) is generally required for the introduction to ensure safety and assess tolerance at the lower turnaround
The Biceps Curl carries a higher potential risk due to loading at full extension; the gap reduces this risk
Seat height should correlate to subject’s height: taller subjects → lower seat, shorter subjects → higher seat
Seat too high → upper arm hovers above pad, only elbow makes contact
Seat too low → elbow doesn’t make contact, upper arm rests at bend of pad
Discomfort in triceps may indicate seat is set too low
Proper seat height should allow full contact of the upper arm with the pad while the chest remains upright against the chest pad
Align the machine’s axis with the elbow joint as closely as possible, though slight misalignment due to individual anatomy is acceptable
Elbow crease should point toward the ceiling, requiring slight external rotation of the humeri
Posture/Get Set:
Use a chest-up posture, leaning into the chest pad
Arms should extend over the pad with elbow creases pointing upward
Instructor lifts the handles from resting position to remove slack; this is the lower turnaround and starting position
Subject grasps handles, palms up, equidistant from center
Instructor supports the movement arm with open hand and cues the subject: “The exercise will begin from this position”
Feet should be flat on the floor or on toes of shoes if shorter
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds:
LOAD UP:
From lower turnaround: chest up, neutral head, relaxed face, breathing freely
Subject gradually applies pressure: elbows down into pad and simultaneous upward pull on handles keeping the wrists flat
Gradual load-up may require slight acceleration to maintain smoothness (same with every exercise)
Instructor quietly observes; positive reinforcement if appropriate
CONCENTRIC/UPPER TURN:
Maintain smooth pace, chest up, head still, wrists flat
Flex elbows tightly to the maximum contraction point
Avoid over-pulling or flexing wrists, which risks unloading the muscle and bringing the arm too close to the face
Pause briefly at the contracted position: 1-2 seconds for first two reps, then 2–3 seconds on rep 3 and beyond
Provide actionable coaching cues during the squeeze phase (e.g. “tighten your elbow joint as hard as you can... keep breathing... [click], ease out”)
ECCENTRIC:
After click, instruct subject to “ease out, do not speed up”
Cue first lower turnaround as “touch and go” or “seamlessly change directions”
Observe carefully to ensure subject maintains control at lower turnaround
Maintain chest up and elbows leveraged firmly into the pad
LOWER TURN:
Lower turnaround is continuous with no pause; “touch and go”
Closely monitor for control to minimize risk at full extension
Reinforce elbow pressure into pad and posture control
INTENSITY:
Set ends when subject can no longer achieve initial degree of elbow flexion with perfect form (i.e. concentric failure)
Only record full reps completed
Partial reps beyond midpoint may be used depending on subject experience and tolerance to intensity
Newer or deconditioned clients should not perform partials initially; focus on fewer full reps with perfect form
Effort must increase as set progresses to maintain pace with rising fatigue
Stimulative mechanical tension comes from increasing voluntary effort under fatigue
NOTES:
Instructor ‘clicks’ only after confirming full contraction and pause has occurred
Subject may mistakenly wait for click to initiate pause; instructor must control this by being assertive and timely
Gap of 1–2 is generally used at first; may be reduced or removed with stronger, more controlled subjects
Full range of motion is not inherently better; individual tolerance should guide range
Elbow should maintain some bend at lower turnaround even with full ROM
Isometric versions (Static Hold or Timed Static Contractions) may be appropriate for injuries such as tennis or golfer’s elbow or for those that cannot lift the 20 pound minimum
For isometrics, instructor assists into and out of position; refer to specific static hold protocol
Lighter weights in early learning may lead to longer sets; this will likely provide diminishing returns due to discomfort occurring prior to stimulus
Over time, look to progress to higher intensity with a more challenging load once form, tolerance, and understanding are established (e.g. 6-sec concentric/6-sec eccentric to failure in ≤10 reps)
Common compensations include lifting elbows off pad or flexing wrists; also likely to shrug in an exaggerated fashion; these must be corrected
Emphasize the goal: maximum muscle tension, not excessive machine movement
Instructor must consistently reinforce proper muscle recruitment and discourage movement compensations
