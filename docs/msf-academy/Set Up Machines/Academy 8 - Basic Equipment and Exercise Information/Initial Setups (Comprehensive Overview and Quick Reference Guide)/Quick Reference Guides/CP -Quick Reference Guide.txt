CP - Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
 • Pectoralis Major – Shoulder horizontal adduction
 • Triceps – Elbow extension
 • Anterior Deltoid – Shoulder horizontal adduction
Synergists
 • Erector Spinae – Spinal extension / anterior pelvic tilt
 • Lower Trapezius – Scapular stabilizer
 • Rotator Cuff – Shoulder stabilizers
Considerations for Setup
 • Provide stool for all clients to support legs (knees should be higher than hips)
 • Provide stool for entry if needed and assist client in safely getting into position
 • Set seat height so upper arm is abducted 30–45° off body, with elbow ~90° or more
 • Ensure handle aligns with hands in this position
 • If seat is too high: elbows will be above hands
 • If seat is too low: elbows significantly below hands
 • Ideal: elbows slightly lower than hands on handles, forearms aligned with movement arm path (forearm will be angled/tracking slightly upward)
 • Place heel of palm on bend of handle for semi-pronated grip (thumbs slightly rotated upward)
 • Determine seat back position by ensuring full elbow extension (or just short of full ext) at upper turnaround without protraction; handles should fully converge; consider the effect of loading when assessing
 • Chest remains up and ribcage rigid through entire ROM
 • Assist handles back with client’s hands on handles to a conservative stretch to determine lower turnaround
 • For compromised shoulders: elbow stays forward of torso midline (less stretch)
 • For healthy shoulders: elbow can be just behind torso midline (more stretch)
 • Record the gap (lower turnaround) on chart
 • Account for compression of padding and flexing of movement arms under load when setting gap and seat back
Posture/Get Set
 • Chest up, hips back, chin tucked (neutral head), shoulders retracted
 • Upper arms abducted 30–45° (elbows out) and stay in that path for duration of set
 • Feet on stool with knees above hips for postural support and stability
 • Avoid dangling feet to minimize shifting and reduce discomfort
 • Hands placed at bend of handles; slight adjustments above or below bend if needed
 • Consider hand pads to reduce pressure
 • Begin movement from lower turnaround
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
 Load Up
 • Maintain posture, neutral head, elbows out
 • “Establish breathing, gradually begin to apply pressure, pushing from chest”
 • Observe closely and silently after instruction
 • Provide brief positive feedback if appropriate (“perfect,” “yes, just like that”)
Concentric Phase
 • Arms converge until handles touch or nearly touch
 • Elbows fully straighten or just short of it at upper turnaround
 • ‘Click’ given precisely at moment handles converge (or furthest reached in concentric)
Eccentric Phase
 • Cue immediately after click: “ease out…do not speed up”
 • No pause at upper turnaround (never on compound pushing movements)
Lower Turnaround
 • Always cue during first eccentric phase to define turnaround expectations
 • Use cues like “touch and go,” “be continuous at the bottom,” “soft touch, do not pause,” etc.
 • Maintain posture, head, pace, and consistent arm/elbow path
Notes
 • Explain gradual load-up if needed: describe ramping pressure or visual analogy of cracking weight stack
 • Gradual load-up may take 3–5 seconds before movement begins
 • No pause in chest press; emphasize exaggerated control at turnarounds
 • Ensure client fully reaches upper turnaround before clicking cue
 • Avoid premature clicks that confuse directional cueing
 • Seat height and back pad influenced by client’s height and arm length
 • Taller clients → lower seat; longer arms → back pad further back
 • Ensure elbow remains slightly lower than hand and follows the movement arm path
 • Movement arms travel out and up; hand and elbow should follow same trajectory
 • Emphasize joint stacking for optimal leverage and natural motion feel
 • With light loads during learning, expect more reps and longer time under tension
 • Be aware of discomfort vs. stimulation; avoid excessive duration that leads to intolerance
 • Progress to heavier loads and shorter sets (≤10 reps) with perfect form and faster reps (~6 sec up / 6 sec down) once client demonstrates control
 • Increase intensity appropriately as client learns Continuous Tension protocol
 • Encourage client to increase effort as fatigue builds to sustain pace
 • Reinforce that rising effort under fatigue is required for mechanical tension
