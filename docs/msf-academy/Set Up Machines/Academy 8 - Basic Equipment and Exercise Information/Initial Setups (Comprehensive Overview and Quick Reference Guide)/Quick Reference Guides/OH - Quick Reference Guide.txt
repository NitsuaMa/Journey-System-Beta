OH - Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Anterior Deltoid – Shoulder Abduction, Shoulder Flexion
Middle Deltoid – Shoulder Abduction, Shoulder Flexion
Triceps – Elbow Extension
Pectoralis Major – Assists in Shoulder Flexion
Synergists
Rectus Abdominis – Posterior Pelvic Tilt (PPT)
Subscapularis – Shoulder Stabilizer
Posterior Deltoid – Shoulder Stabilizer
Trapezius – Scapular Rotation and Stabilizer (Scapulohumeral Rhythm)
Considerations for Setup
Evaluate if subject needs a stool for entry and/or leg support
Confirm seat height using the standard gap of 2 on the weight stack
Seat height is correct if subject’s arms hang naturally at the sides then with just an elbow bend, the hands land near the ends of the handles
For compromised shoulder joints, consider lowering seat and/or increasing gap to reduce ROM
Ensure subject uses a neutral grip, with hands near bottom of the handles
Note that the handles are angled downward in the starting position and the forearm is perpendicular to the handle.  This will ensure the proper arm path as the movement arm will follow an arc
Instruct subject to maintain a posterior pelvic tilt (PPT) with abdominal contraction
Account for pad compression and arm flex under load—adjust as needed
Posture / Get Set
Confirm PPT and explain its purpose: abdominal engagement and lumbar safety
Ensure head is in neutral position, chin slightly tucked, gaze downward
Hands near ends of handles, palms facing in; hand pads optional
Upper arms should be in the scapular plane (30°–40° anterior to frontal plane)
Scapulae should remain free—not forced into retraction or protraction
Feet must rest firmly on a support stool—no leg dangling
Movement should begin from lower turnaround position
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Confirm starting posture: hips back, feet supported, PPT active, neutral head, scapular plane alignment
Cue a gradual load-up through palms—emphasize building pressure over 3–5 seconds
Provide initial observational feedback—reinforce proper beginning technique
Confirm force vector is perpendicular to angled handle (requires slight forward push)
Maintain consistent elbow path both concentrically and eccentrically
Ensure full arm extension at top—some natural scapular elevation is expected
Instruct to continue full ROM before clicking at upper turnaround—click timing is critical
Immediately post-click, cue: “Ease out, do not speed up”
Establish first eccentric expectation: “Touch and go,” “Be continuous,” or “Do not pause”
Confirm smooth, continuous pace—no stopping at turnarounds
Reinforce consistent posture, head position, pace, and arm path
Monitor and cue increased effort to match rising fatigue—stimulative tension is effort-dependent
Notes
Use analogies to explain gradual load-up (e.g., finding 101 lbs to crack a 100 lb stack)
Emphasize continuous effort—no pause, but slow transitions at turnarounds
Ensure full elbow extension before cueing upper turnaround click
Instructors should not cue the click prematurely—this can shorten ROM and create confusion
Adjust seat height and gap based on subject height and arm length
If subject experiences discomfort in upper range, limit ROM and use a selector pin to establish a consistent end range
During early stages of Continuous Tension learning, expect higher reps due to lighter load
Avoid excessive time under tension if it becomes more uncomfortable than stimulative
Progress to “faster” reps (6s up / 6s down) and heavier load targeting failure in ≤10 reps once control is established
