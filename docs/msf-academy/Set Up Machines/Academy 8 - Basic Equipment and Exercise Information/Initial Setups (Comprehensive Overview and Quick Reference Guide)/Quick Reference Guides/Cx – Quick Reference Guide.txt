Cervical Extension – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles:
Trapezius (Upper Back/Neck Extensors)
Semispinalis Muscle Group
Splenius Capitis
Longissimus Muscle Group
 → All contribute to Cervical Extension
Synergists:
Erector Spinae – Spinal Extension / Anterior Pelvic Tilt
Multifidus – Spinal Stability
Quadratus Lumborum (QL) – Spinal Extension / Anterior Pelvic Tilt
Setup Considerations:
1. Seat Height:
Taller clients → lower seat; shorter clients → higher seat
Seat height affects vertical alignment of the machine’s axis with the cervical spine (typically C3–C5)
2. Seat Depth:
Most clients should sit mid-seat, not fully back, to ensure horizontal alignment with machine’s axis
Instructor must pull movement arm (head pad) back before client sits
3. Back Pad (Not required to use):
If used - light contact with client’s mid-back — do not push them forward
4. Practical Alignment (Most Important):
Back of head must sit flush and securely in the recess of the head pad
Too low = pad rides up over head
Too high = pad misses base of skull
Any shifting during execution = potential misalignment → reposition while unloaded
No gap required for this movement; full ROM based on comfort is acceptable
5. Instructor Role:
Control movement arm at all times during setup
Adjust client’s position to fit the head securely into the pad
Typical start weight = 20 lbs (lightest)
Posture / Get Set:
Upright posture with chest up, slight chin elevation
Feet flat (or heels supported), hands on front handles, elbows in
Cue: “Sit tall…chin up/down…scoot forward” as needed
Instructor brings head pad to client’s head, assessing flush contact
Stand to the side of client to check alignment
Hand off the load only after confirming zero movement; say “That is yours”
If unstable, cue: “Push harder,” “Hold it still,” etc.
Execution & Turnarounds:
Eccentric (Lowering):
Cue: “Let the head come forward…maintain flush contact with the pad”
Ribcage may need to drop slightly to maintain smooth contact
Cue: “Go as far forward as is comfortable”
Seamless change of direction at bottom (no pause)
Helpful cues:
“Push squarely into the pad”
“Keep your head in the path of the machine”
Concentric (Lifting):
Cue: “Lift your head/chin while arching your back and lifting your chest”
Encourage full extension of cervical, thoracic, and lumbar spine
Cue ideas:
“Follow it all the way, back and down”
“Head to butt, butt to head”
“Draw a rainbow with the top of your head”
Upper Turnaround:
Pause only after maximal, safe extension has been achieved
Trainer clicks only after movement stops and full contraction is reached
DO NOT cue client to wait for the click to stop; click follows correct completion
Important:
NEVER train to failure
Stop the set before technical failure
Prioritize control and safety over intensity
Progress conservatively and gradually over time
Notes & Cautions:
The cervical spine is less conditioned to external load → greater injury risk
Emphasize rotary movement (not linear push/pull)
Remind: “Lift/lower the chin” or “Arc the head”
Avoid letting client push straight back or slide on pad
Instructor must perfect handoff technique
Ideal rep range: ≤8 reps, especially early in training
Isometric Option (Static Hold or Timed Static Contraction):
Safe alternative for injured or deconditioned clients
Position just beyond neutral with chin slightly elevated
Cue: “Push back and down into the pad”
Instructor loads and unloads weight; no motion should occur
