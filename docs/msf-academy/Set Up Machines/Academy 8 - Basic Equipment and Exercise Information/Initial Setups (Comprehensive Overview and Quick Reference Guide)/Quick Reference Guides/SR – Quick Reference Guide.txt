Simple Row – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Posterior Deltoid (Rear Delt): Shoulder horizontal abduction
Rhomboids (Upper Back): Scapular retraction/adduction and downward rotation
Trapezius (Upper Back): Scapular retraction/adduction and downward rotation
Synergists
Infraspinatus (Rotator Cuff): Shoulder horizontal abduction
Erector Spinae (Spinal Erectors): Stabilizers, spinal extension/anterior pelvic tilt (chest-up posture)
Considerations for Setup
Determine seat height based on subject height: taller subjects likely need a lower seat, shorter subjects a higher seat
Fine-tune seat height so upper arm slopes down slightly (shoulder higher than elbow)
Subject’s head should be near the overhead padded “work box” when seated
For the chest pad, the theoretical alignment would have shoulders directly under axes of rotation
Practical alignment takes priority: elbow should land in at least the middle of the concave arm pads, and pads must remain flush through movement
Adjust chest pad based on arm length: longer arms = chest pad further from arm pads; shorter arms = chest pad in closer
Determine final chest pad setting based on interplay of arm length and torso size to ensure arm pads remain flush throughout
May require a gap for subjects with a wider frame; in some cases, the wider subject will need assistance for entry/exit by pulling movement arms apart
Adjust vertical handle height for longer arms to ensure elbows align with center of arm pads; hands do not hang from handle but rather wedge against them to create force couple with elbow
If external rotation is limited, advise client to “cap” the ends of the handles with palms facing themselves for comfort
Posture / Get Set
Chest up, hips close to chest pad so abdomen is against the pad
Head neutral, arms inside pads, hands on handles
Ensure abdomen contacts chest pad to allow ribcage lift and facilitate both thoracic extension and scapular retraction
Begin at lower turnaround with emphasized chest-up posture
Feet on floor; for shorter legs/higher seats, feet back on toe of shoe
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Load Up: From lower turnaround, chest up, neutral head, arms in place, establish breathing, gradually apply pressure by driving out through elbows
After instruction, trainer quietly observes load up; offer affirmation if done correctly (e.g., “perfect” or “yes, just like that”)
Concentric / Upper Turn: Subject lifts chest and draws shoulder blades together until full contraction is achieved
Instructor clicks once desired duration in contracted position is reached
Eccentric: Immediately follow click with cue “ease out” or “do not speed up”
Lower Turnaround: Cue with “touch and go,” “be continuous,” or “seamlessly change directions”
Maintain chest-up posture using abdomen to leverage off chest pad
Execute at a continuous pace with a brief pause (~1 second) on first two reps and a 2–3 second squeeze from third rep onward
Avoid vague cues like “squeeze”—instead give actionable instructions on HOW to contract
Example verbiage:
“Leveraging off your abdomen, lift your chest and draw your shoulder blades together as hard as you can… keep breathing, keep still, [click], ease out, do not speed up”
“Keeping your head still, attempt to wrap your shoulder blades around your spine… there, lock it in place, [click], ease out”
Maintain consistent posture, head position, pace, and arm pad contact throughout
Only full range-of-motion reps with proper contraction count toward recorded total
Instructor may choose to continue set beyond concentric failure if under strict control and tension remains stimulative
For brand-new clients, full-range reps without failure may be sufficient based on form, tolerance, and experience level of client
Effort must increase throughout the set to maintain pace as fatigue rises; mechanical tension is created by matching effort to fatigue
Notes
Always point out overhead padded mechanism on entry and exit; cue “watch your head” for safety
Expect variability in client ROM; use first 2–3 reps to assess and establish individual expectations
Encourage full scapular retraction; avoid leaning back to move movement arms further
Simple Row is suitable for clients with hand, wrist, or elbow limitations and useful for improving posture
May pair with pulldown to complement upper back engagement, particularly due to lack of scapular retraction in pulldown
For clients with shoulder concerns, TSC may be appropriate:
Pin weight stack with movement arms at approximate midpoint to create an immovable load
Cue effort progression: 50% effort (30 sec), 75% effort (30 sec), all-out effort (30 sec) = 90 sec total; may use shorter durations to introduce
Helps strengthen shoulder and may eventually allow transition to dynamic execution
Estimated carryover of strength ~15° above and below static position
During early sessions with lighter weight, clients may perform more reps due to learn the movement/protocol with lower intensity
High rep volume may lead to discomfort before stimulation; Over time, look to progress to heavier weights (~10 reps max) as soon as control and tolerance to intensity is demonstrated
Aim for 6-second positive / 6-second negative cadence with appropriate intensity once form is consistent
