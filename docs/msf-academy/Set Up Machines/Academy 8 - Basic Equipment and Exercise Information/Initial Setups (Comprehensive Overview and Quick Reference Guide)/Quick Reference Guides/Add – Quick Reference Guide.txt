Adduction – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
Adductor Brevis (Inner Thigh) – Hip horizontal adduction
Adductor Longus (Inner Thigh) – Hip horizontal adduction
Adductor Magnus (Inner Thigh) – Hip horizontal adduction
Pectineus (Inner Thigh) – Hip horizontal adduction
Gracilis (Inner Thigh) – Hip horizontal adduction
Considerations for Setup
Seat back typically standardized at position 6 or 7
Instructor must assist client with entry and exit; take full responsibility for safety during setup
Main setup consideration is establishing a conservative ROM for intro using custom gap for every subject
Clear main stack (and accessory stack if needed) before setting the gap
Ensure movement arms are far enough apart, then have the client take a seat
Begin bringing movement arms together and cue client to lift left leg into place, then right leg
Slowly allow movement arms apart, maintaining contact with leg/hand to avoid sudden movement
Ask client to indicate when a slight stretch is felt on the inner thigh
Set selector pin into desired main stack weight and record gap
Instructor should use judgment if client requests more stretch; assess risk/benefit
Posture/Get Set
Sit back, butt back, neutral head
Feet and ankles relaxed
Hands on handles for balance, not anchoring
Encourage relaxed upper body with optional abdominal contraction
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Load Up
From lower turnaround, establish neutral head, hands on handles, and steady breathing
Gradually begin pressing knees together
Instructor observes closely; affirm with “good” or “mmm, hmm” when performed correctly
Concentric Phase / Upper Turnaround
Smoothly drive knees together into upper turnaround
Pause in the contracted position for about 1-2 seconds on first two reps, then 2–3 seconds from rep three onward
Instructor clicks at the exact moment the contraction duration ends
Eccentric Phase
Immediately after the click, cue “ease out, do not speed up”
Establish the expectation for the first lower turnaround during the eccentric: “touch and go,” “be continuous,” or “seamlessly change directions as the weights touch”
Always provide a cue on the first eccentric phase of the set
Lower Turnaround
Continuous movement at the bottom—“touch and go” without pausing
Maintain same controlled pace throughout
Execution
Continuous cadence with only a “brief but definite” pause in the contracted position (1-2 seconds for first two reps, 2–3 seconds from third rep on)
Do not rely solely on the word “squeeze”—teach the client HOW to contract
Example verbiage:
“Continue to drive your knees together…keep breathing, lock it in place, [click], ease out…keep that pace.”
Only full reps are counted/recorded on chart
Set may continue after full ROM is lost if reps remain under strict control and client is advanced and/or tolerant of higher levels of intensity
Set continuation after concentric failure should be assessed with Studio Leader or MSF Corporate
For newer clients, a few full reps may be sufficient, specifically on Adduction; failure is not required in initial sessions
Encourage consistent pace while increasing effort as fatigue builds
Effort must rise to counter fatigue for mechanical tension to remain stimulative
Notes
Instructor must remain fully responsible for client safety during entry/exit
Entire movement arm setup must be under instructor control with clear and timely instruction
Always begin with a conservative stretch to avoid vulnerable positions
Gradual increases in stretch may be warranted over time if strength and tolerance improve
Increased ROM should only occur under instructor supervision and with Studio Leader
Cue clients to press through knees, even if knees do not directly contact the thigh pads
Referring to the knees simplifies instruction even though the femur is the acting lever
Special Populations / Conservative Approaches
Clients with hip replacements or related issues may not tolerate this movement pattern
Most conservative approach is to avoid the exercise, but strengthening may provide benefit
Alternative: Timed Static Contraction (TSC)
Use weight too heavy to lift and set movement arms at midpoint
Instruct static pressing against thigh pads in progressive intensity stages:
30 seconds @ 50% effort
30 seconds @ 75% effort
30 seconds @ 100% effort
TSC performed weekly over time builds strength and may allow eventual dynamic movement; expect ~15 degrees of ROM carryover in strength
Progression Considerations
Continuous Tension protocol typically starts with lighter weight, higher reps, and longer sets
Excessive time under load can lead to discomfort before stimulus is achieved
Over time, look to target perfect form with 6-second positive and 6-second negative reps
Aim for heavier weights that lead to failure in 10 or fewer reps when appropriate as this will act to minimize discomfort
If client demonstrates sufficient control, intensity may be progressed more quickly
