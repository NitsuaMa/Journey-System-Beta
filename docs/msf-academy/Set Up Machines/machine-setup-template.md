# Master Machine Setup & Biomechanics Template
### A Standardized Protocol for Adding New or Legacy Equipment to the HIT System

Use this template whenever a new machine is introduced to the studio or when documenting a legacy machine. This ensures that every piece of equipment is onboarded with the same high-intensity, continuous-tension, and biomechanically rigorous standards used throughout the existing 20-machine lineup.

---

## Part 1: How to Extract Machine Biomechanics (The Evaluator's Guide)

Before filling out the template for a new machine, the evaluating strength coach/biomechanist must run the machine through this 5-step diagnostic process:

1. **Identify the Functional Axis of Rotation**:
   * Locate the physical pivot point of the machine's movement arm. 
   * Determine the corresponding anatomical joint axis (e.g., lateral epicondyle of the femur for Leg Extension; glenohumeral joint center for Pullover/Lateral Raise). 
   * Define how the client's body must be positioned (via seat height or pad placement) to align these two axes.

2. **Establish the Passive Tension & Range of Motion (ROM) Baseline**:
   * Sit in the machine and determine where the muscle is at its safest maximum stretch.
   * Identify what prevents further movement in the stretched position (e.g., joint structure, muscle tightness, or the weight stack bottoming out).
   * Define a baseline **Weight Stack Gap** (typically 2 to 6 plates) to create a safe "buffer" at the end of the eccentric range, preventing the plates from landing and unloading tension.

3. **Map the Leverages & Force Profile**:
   * Does the machine feel easiest at the start, midpoint, or fully contracted position?
   * If leverage is poorest at the start (concentric), a **Trainer-Assisted Handoff** is required. Define the handoff grip, body positioning, and verbal cue.
   * If the exercise is a single-joint (rotary) movement, identify where the 1–3 second "Pause/Squeeze" should be implemented at full contraction.
   * If it is a compound (linear) movement, note that a "Touch-and-Go" cadence must be maintained at the turnaround to prevent unloading.

4. **Define the Anchor Points (Pelvic & Torso Stability)**:
   * What forces will act on the client's body during heavy loading? (e.g., upward force in Pullover/Seated Dip; backward/forward forces in Chest Press/Row).
   * Establish necessary restraints (lap belts, shoulder pads, chest pads, or active gripping of handles) to lock the pelvis and torso into place, preventing secondary muscle compensation.

5. **Anticipate Common Compensations (The Warning Signs)**:
   * Watch for joint shearing, shrugging (trapezius takeover), pelvic rotation, hip lifting, or cervical hyperextension.
   * Turn these observations into 1-2 non-negotiable **Critical Alignment Checkpoints** for the instructing coach.

## Part 3: Example of a Completed Template (for Reference)

### Machine Name: Lateral Raise (Middle Deltoid)

### 1. Target Musculature
*   **Primary Muscles**: Lateral Deltoid (shoulder abduction).
*   **Secondary Muscles**: Anterior and Posterior Deltoid (stabilizers during abduction).
*   **Synergists / Stabilizers**: Upper Trapezius (scapular elevation - *must be minimized*), Rotator Cuff (supraspinatus initiates abduction).

### 2. The Universal Baseline
*   **Seat Height**: Set seat height so that the physical pivot axis of the machine's rotating arms is level with the center of the client's shoulder joints (glenohumeral joint) when they are seated upright.
*   **Pad & Axis Alignment**: The client's upper arms must rest flush against the lateral arm pads, with elbows bent at 90 degrees.
*   **Restraints & Anchoring**: Secure seat belt tightly across the pelvis. Instruct the client to grip the hand handles lightly—do not squeeze aggressively, as this can over-recruit the forearms and biceps.
*   **Starting Weight Stack Gap**: Standardized gap of 2 to allow a full, safe stretch at the bottom without the plates touching.

### 3. Body Type Adjustments (The Variables)
*   **Shorter Stature / Short Limbs**:
    *   *Seat Adjustment*: Raise the seat to align shoulders with the axis.
    *   *Pad/Handle Placement*: Ensure the elbows are centered on the arm pads. If arms are too short to reach the handles naturally, focus solely on keeping the elbows flush against the pad and ignore the handles.
*   **Taller Stature / Long Limbs**:
    *   *Seat Adjustment*: Lower the seat to align shoulders with the axis.
    *   *Special Setup Notes*: If the client has a very wide shoulder span, ensure their neck is not compressed or pushed forward by the upper framework of the machine.
*   **Limited Mobility / Joint Constraints**:
    *   *Shoulder Impingement / AC Joint Issues*: Increase the weight stack gap to limit the downward range of motion. Instruct the client to stop the upward concentric phase slightly below 90 degrees of shoulder abduction to prevent painful impingement.

### 4. Critical Alignment Checkpoints
1.  **Shoulder-to-Axis Alignment**: Confirm that the center of the shoulder joint does not shrug up above the machine's axis of rotation before starting a repetition.
2.  **Spinal Posture & Pelvis**: Ensure the client maintains a tight abdominal wall (posterior pelvic tilt) to keep the chest down and prevent the lumbar spine from hyperextending to "cheat" the weight up.

### 5. Execution & Cadence Notes
*   **The Handoff / Load-Up Protocol**: A handoff is generally not required for this machine, but a very slow, patient "load-up" (denting the pads) is mandatory to crack the weight stack over 3-5 seconds without momentum.
*   **Continuous Tension Cadence**: Enforce a strict 6-second concentric sweep and 6-second eccentric lowering phase.
*   **Turnaround Protocols**:
    *   *Upper Turnaround*: Pause and hold the fully abduced position (arms parallel to the floor) for a 2-3 second "squeeze" from the third rep onward. Cue: *"Keep the shoulders down, lead with the elbows, hold and squeeze."*
    *   *Lower Turnaround*: Seamlessly reverse direction just before the plates touch at the bottom (gap of 2). Absolutely no bouncing or resting at the bottom.
