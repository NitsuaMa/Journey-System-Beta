Pullover - Gap 6 – handoff in the contracted position (UT)
SETUP
Set seat, set back pad (^ for most), set weight, set elbow pads (M for most)
“Have a seat, all the way back.”
Seat belt
“You will be primarily working your Lats on this exercise, but also most of the muscles in your upper body including your abs, chest, shoulders, and arms.  Your lats are the large muscles on the outer sides of your back (visually indicate).”
Prepare to assist movement arm down into contracted position.
HANDOFF + FIRST ECCENTRIC
“Bring your arms together in front of you and watch your shoulders as I bring the movement arm down.  Place your upper arms and elbows on the pads.  Hands on the bar.”
“We will be pushing through the elbows with no effort from the hands.  Hands just rest on the bar.”
Handoff the movement arm - “That is yours”
“Now you’re going to raise the movement arm smoothly keeping the pressure through the elbows.”
“Raise the movement arm up far enough that you feel the weight stack touch and then smoothly change directions by pushing down through the elbows.”
LOWER TURN + FIRST CONCENTRIC + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Good!”  (NOTE: if they did not properly execute the lower turn, instruct/correct on the eccentric of the next rep).
“Now, as you are pushing down through the elbows, begin to tighten your abdomen and draw your elbows in.”
“When your upper arms reach the sides of your ribcage, I’d like you to pause…right there…abdomen tight, elbows in, keep still, [click], ease out.”  (NOTE: This is the “squeeze” technique that we refer to.  Recall that on the first two reps there is a brief pause of 1 to 2 seconds.  From the third rep on, there is a “squeeze” of 2 to 3 seconds.  The squeeze is just a slightly longer pause.  It’s used to establish control and to help create awareness of the working muscles.  The same cueing can be used for both the pause and the squeeze.  The duration of the pause on the first two reps is shorter with shorter phrasing.  The instruction on the squeeze is slightly longer with slightly drawn out instruction.)

SETUP

HANDOFF + FIRST ECCENTRIC
“Grab the handles with an underhand grip, close to the inside of the handles.”
Handoff - “Now pull down on those handles, increase your force…that is yours.”
“From here I’d like you to slowly raise the movement arm until your arms completely straighten or the weights touch.  Whichever happens first, you will change directions seamlessly.”

Compound Row - Gap 2 – handoff near the contracted position (UT), chest up
SETUP
Set seat (chest pad), set chest pad height (^ or v), set weight, consider handle width (M for most).
“Have a seat, [watch your head], chest up and leaning forward into the chest pad.”
“This exercise will target most of the muscles in your back but most specifically your upper back and lats.  You’ll certainly feel your arms working too, but understand that our primary target is the upper back.”
If the client indicates that they are not feeling their upper back or if it is still a relatively new movement for them we can demonstrate with the following: “Place your hands on your hips and now try and touch your elbows together behind your back.”  Instructor should also demonstrate so they can see.  “Do you feel how your shoulder blades are congesting your upper back?  That’s the feeling we are trying to create on this exercise.  This takes practice though so just trust that even if you don’t feel these muscles working, the body has no choice but to use them for this movement.”
Ensure that both handles are in the proper slot and prepare your position to assist the movement arm back to the contracted position (UT).  Trainer will need to assume a wide stance and grab the very bottom of the movement arm with one hand and the pronated handle with the other.  Use your bodyweight by shifting back while pulling movement arm back.
HANDOFF + FIRST ECCENTRIC
“Reach out and grab the upright (neutral) handle on the right.” Again, ensure it enters the proper slot (M for most).
“And now this one on the left.”  We will essentially ‘present’ the neutral handle to them by grabbing the pronated handle and beginning to pull back.
“OK, let’s pull back together.”
Handoff - “Now, chest up, pull your shoulder blades together, increase your force…that is yours.”
“From here I’d like you to slowly begin to lower the movement arm until your arms completely straighten or the weights touch.  Whichever happens first, you will immediately change directions.”

Simple Row - No gap (depending on width/mobility) – starts at LT, chest up
SETUP
Set seat, set chest pad (most will be 4 or 5), set weight
“WATCH YOUR HEAD, have a seat, chest up with abdomen against the pad”
“We are targeting the upper back and the backs of the shoulders on this exercise.  We will even involve some of the rotator cuff muscles.”
“This is a great exercise for your posture and your shoulder mobility.”
“Bring your arms up so your elbows land inside these pads.”  (NOTE: there is a good chance the trainer will have to assist on both sides to ensure pads sit flush against the upper arm/elbow).
“Place your hands on the handles with your palms facing you.”  (NOTE: it may be helpful to try different hand placements along the handle.  Even lightly ‘capping’ the ends of the handle may provide more ROM for clients with limited ER).
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK, from here, keep your chest up, head still, breathing freely, and begin to gradually apply pressure out through the elbow pads.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Yes!  Now keep lifting your ribcage, leveraging off of your abdomen, and drive your elbow behind you.  Try and draw your shoulder blades together as tightly as possible.”
Ensure client is not leaning back but rather lifting their ribcage, keeping their abdomen against the chest pad.
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing, keep your head still, see if you can get even tighter between your shoulder blades…good…keep rigidly still…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has fully exhausted their ROM.  This will vary dramatically between individuals.  Some will fully retract their scapulae but others may not appear to retract even slightly.  For them, this will be more of a posterior deltoid exercise.  Look to improve over time.

Biceps - Gap 1 or 2 (depending on mobility and belt tightness) – starts at LT
SETUP
Set seat, set weight
“Have a seat, arms on top of the pad with chest against the pad.”
“We are targeting the biceps on this movement.”
“OK, reach out for the handles and take an underhand grip (palms up).”  The instructor will have to assist the movement arm up to the starting position (LT) because of the gap.  We are taking the ‘slack out’ first so there is no delay when the client engages and to ensure symmetrical hand placement.
“Your wrists will stay flat throughout this movement [demonstrate].”
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK, the movement will start from here [instructor supporting movement arm], keep your head still, breathe freely, and begin to drive your elbows down into the pad as you pull the handles up toward you.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Good…now keep in mind we are not necessarily pulling the handles up as far as we can but rather just getting as tight as we can get at the elbow joint...try and squeeze the crease of your elbows.”
Ensure the client will not pull the movement arm into their face/head.  We may need to stop the movement short of an apparent full contraction if they are hyper flexible.
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing…see if you can get even tighter at the elbow…good…now, keep tight…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to ensure the client has fully exhausted their ROM.  Stay attentive to the possibility that the handles will come near their face.  The ROM will vary between individuals.  Some will be able to completely close (flex) their elbow joint and bring the handles up all the way to their face.  The trainer needs to protect them.  Others will lack mobility and/or they will have large enough biceps that they are prevented from fully closing the joint.  Wherever that range of motion ends is where the pause/squeeze will take place and where we will consider a full rep.

Chest Press - Custom gap — starts at LT, chest up
SETUP
Set seat, set back pad, set weight, foot stool
“Have a seat, all the way back, chest up.”
“This exercise targets your chest and triceps primarily.  But you will also be using the fronts of your shoulders quite a bit.”
“Place the heel of your hand right at the base of your thumb at the bend of the handle” [instructor points to their own hand if necessary].  This hand position results in a semi pronated grip that allows the angle of the wrist to approximate the angle of the upper arm, which tends to be a more joint friendly position overall at the shoulder, elbow, and wrist.
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“Now you are going to keep your head and ribcage absolutely still with your chest up throughout the movement.  Elbows out so they are behind your hands.  Establish your breathing and begin to gradually apply pressure from your armpits out through the handles.”
Observe closely, watching for a proper gradual load up.
“Good!  Now, SLIGHTLY speed up in order to stay smooth.  Keep that pace…keep that posture.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
UPPER TURN
Watch closely to ensure the concentric has been fully completed.  Handles should touch or come close with arms straightened, depending on setup.
“Chest up, keep breathing, drive those handles together…good, [click], ease out.”
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Head still…elbows out…keep that pace.”
“Now as we approach the bottom of the movement, you are going to barely let those plates touch and seamlessly change directions.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
“Perfect!  Make it look the same every time!”

Overhead - Gap 2 — starts at LT, abdomen tight (PPT)
SETUP
Set seat, set weight, foot stool
“Have a seat, all the way back.”
“This exercise targets your shoulders and triceps primarily.  But you will also be using your abdomen for stability.”
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“Now tighten down your abdomen so that the middle of your back rounds into the back pad.  Make sure you are breathing freely.  Elbows out.  Begin to gradually push from your shoulders into the handles.”
Observe closely, looking for the gradual load up.
“Good!  Now, SLIGHTLY speed up in order to stay smooth.  Keep that pace.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
“Keep your abdomen tight and reach as far as you are able.”
UPPER TURN
Watch closely to ensure the concentric has been fully completed.  Arms should completely straighten (or as close to straight as client can get).  There should be a brief but definite stillness at the top of the ROM.
“Straighten those arms, keep breathing, good, [click], ease out.”
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Keep your head still, keep your pace.”
“Now as we approach the bottom of the movement, you are going to barely let those plates touch and smoothly and continuously change directions.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
“Good! Just like that!”

Seated Dip - No gap — starts at LT w/ chest up, PPT at UT
SETUP
Set seat, lift back pad out of the way, set weight, foot stool
“Have a seat, not quite all the way back.” (NOTE: Recall that the larger someone is the further back in the seat they will be.  Smaller clients will be further forward).
Seat belt
“This exercise targets your chest and triceps primarily.  But you will also be using your abdomen as you push your arms straight.”
“Lean forward for me and place your hands near the ends of the handles.”  We will offer the choice of either a neutral hand position or pronated.
Lower the back pad down to make contact with the mid to upper back.  This should force the client to lean forward.
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“Make sure you are breathing freely.  Head down into neutral position.  Elbows out.  Begin to leverage off of the back pad as you gradually build pressure down into the handles.”
Observe closely, looking for the gradual load up.
“Yes!  Now, SLIGHTLY speed up in order to stay smooth…keep that pace.”  (NOTE: Recall that if the client properly executes a gradual load up, they will most likely need to slightly speed up once they are moving.  Otherwise the pace will be too slow and the movement will become segmented).
“Tighten down your abdomen as you begin to straighten your arms.”
UPPER TURN
Watch closely to ensure the concentric has been fully completed.  Arms should completely straighten (or as close to straight as client can get).  There should be a brief but definite ‘stillness’ at the end of the ROM.
“Straighten those arms further, tighten down harder, keep breathing, good, [click], ease out…don’t speed up.”
FIRST ECCENTRIC + LOWER TURN
“Keep your head still, keep your pace.”
“Now keep lowering until you feel those weights just barely touch and change directions as smoothly as you can.”
Let the client change directions, observe closely.
“mmm, hmm! Just like that…push hard, move slow.”

Lateral Raise - No gap (depending on width) – starts at LT, abdomen tight (PPT)
SETUP
Set seat, set back pad (^ for most), set weight, open handle to allow seating, foot stool
“Have a seat, all the way back, [close handle] arms inside the pads, hands open on the handles.”  (NOTE: Most clients will be position one or two on the handle, make sure both handles are at the same setting).
“We are targeting the sides of the shoulders (middle deltoid) on this movement.”
LOAD UP + FIRST CONCENTRIC
“OK, from here, tighten down your midsection so the middle of your back rounds into the back pad.  Notice that your ribcage is drawn down, try to avoid lifting it by keeping tension in your abdomen.  Head still, breathing freely, and begin to gradually apply pressure out through the elbow pads.”
Observe the gradual load up.
“Mmm, hmm…focus on driving OUT through the elbows, leveraging off the back pad.  And we’ll drive out until the upper arm is just about parallel to the floor.”  (NOTE: ROM will depend on mobility of the client.  Some may be able to abduct  above parallel to the floor.  Realize that further isn’t necessarily better as the tension will often shift more directly to the trapezius and neck muscles.  Also, a slightly shorter ROM that does not reach a parallel to the floor position is still quite effective).
Ensure client is not arching their back and shrugging but rather driving out through the elbows by leveraging off of the back pad.  A foot stool will assist in keeping a more stable positioning.
UPPER TURN + PAUSE/SQUEEZE
“Keep breathing, head still, THERE…good, now keep rigidly still…[click] ease out.”
Watch closely to determine where the optimal ROM (UT) will be for each client.  As mentioned above this can vary drastically between individuals.  The instructor will use verbal cues to either pause them or encourage more ROM.  Then the ‘click’ will follow.
