Lumbar – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles:
Erector Spinae (Spinal Erectors/Spinal Extensors) – Spinal Extension/Anterior Pelvic Tilt
Multifidus – Spinal Extension/Spinal Stability
Quadratus Lumborum (QL’s) – Spinal Extension/Anterior Pelvic Tilt
Synergists:
Gluteus Maximus – Stabilizer
Latissimus Dorsi – Spinal Extension
Trapezius – Stabilizer, Scapular Retraction/Adduction and Downward Rotation
Considerations for Setup:
If accessory weight stack will be used, it must be set prior to client sitting
Move carriage all the way back and place pin in main stack to lock movement arm
Determine seat pad height by:
Practical Alignment - look for the back pad to land on as much of the shoulder blade area as possible
Theoretical Alignment - use iliac crest alignment with top of the roller pad
Monitor top-heaviness that may add leverage and reduce effectiveness (i.e. shoulder blades above back pad) - seat might be too high
Raise and move femur restraint to clear space for footplate adjustment
Position footplate so knee joint is slightly higher than hip joint, creating a downward femur slope
Place seatbelt across hip crease after feet are on footplate and femur restraint is in place
Ensure femur restraint pads straddle patella evenly; upper pad on femur, lower on tibia
Pads should be snug with slight wiggle room and angle congruent with femur and tibia
If pads are not congruent to knee, adjust foot placement on footplate; raise foot if knee angle is too severe
Adjust footplate as needed after foot repositioning
Secure femur restraint snugly after all lower body alignment is complete
Establish lower turnaround by removing main stack pin and carefully pulling movement arm and subject forward
Adjust lower turnaround conservatively based on client size and consider any history of low back issues
Most benefits are derived from the extension phase; avoid deep flexion under load
Recheck and tighten femur pads after setting lower turnaround as subject will have likely slid back some
Posture/Get Set:
Cue: “Chin tucked, chest up, establish your breathing and gradually begin to build pressure into the back pad by lifting the ribcage”
Prevent lifting the head and breath hold during effort initiation
Deliver cue slowly and clearly for client processing and implementation
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds:
Load Up:
Cue gradual pressure into back pad
Observe quietly and reinforce with “perfect” or “yes, just like that” if appropriate
Check for overly emphasized leg drive, especially if knees are not higher than hips
Concentric:
Cue: “Chest up, back arched…” or “Lift your chest and arch your back”
Use descriptive language to guide the process of spinal extension
Avoid relying solely on potentially confusing terms like “extend your spine” or “squeeze”
Upper Turn:
Observe range and tolerance at end stop
Confirm full range achieved if tolerable.  Watch the stopper abutt the frame
Pause briefly at upper turnaround for 1-2 seconds; beginning with rep 3, implement 2–3 second squeeze
Cue: “Keep arching, keep breathing, [click], ease out”
Always instruct how to squeeze, do not just say “squeeze”
Eccentric:
Click at the end of the squeeze duration
Cue: “Ease out, and do not speed up” or similar
Use first eccentric of every exercise to set expectations for lower turn
Lower Turn:
Cue for lower turnaround: “Touch and go,” “be continuous at the bottom,” “barely touch, barely start,” “seamlessly change direction,” or “soft touch, do not pause”
As with all instruction, timing is critical; the cue needs to come before the turn
Overall Execution Notes:
Never take lumbar extension to failure; extra precaution with new subjects or those with low back issues
Posture and Path are essentially controlled due to restraints, which makes this movement easier to learn
Reinforce reminders for head position and spinal extension
Emphasize maintaining a consistent and controlled pace
Client must increase effort across set as fatigue increases
Ensure every rep is completed with exacting control
Notes:
Screen all clients for contraindications and movement limitations during consultation (review intake/demographics packet and discuss)
Avoid lumbar extension altogether for those with known orthopedic issues unless medically cleared
Consider a conservative starting point using Timed Static Contraction (TSC):
Pin heavy weight to prevent movement - this is a static variation
Position movement arm just beyond midpoint of ROM (slightly biased toward upper turnaround)
Cue static effort progression: 30 seconds @ 50%, 30 seconds @ 75%, 30 seconds @ 100%;  NOTE: may need to introduce even more conservatively with shorter duration (15 seconds vs. 30 at each level)
Perform at least weekly for several months for strength improvements
TSC has a reported ~15° carryover range; may enable future dynamic use
Variability in setup can impact performance (e.g., footplate, femur restraint, seatbelt tension)
Maintain setup consistency and document all “fine tuning” adjustments
If client reports discomfort from the roller pad:
Consider that it may be from the client’s waistline of pants or belt
Evaluate footplate height; knees too high may increase pressure
Raise or lower seat to adjust roller contact point as needed
Record all adjustments for consistency in future sessions
