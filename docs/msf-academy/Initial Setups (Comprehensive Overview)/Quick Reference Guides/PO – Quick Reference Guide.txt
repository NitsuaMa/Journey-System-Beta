Considerations for Setup
Seat back has two positions: most clients will use the "up and back" position; smaller clients may use "down and forward"
Start with seat height based on subject height: taller = lower seat, shorter = higher seat
Align machine’s axis of rotation slightly above subject’s shoulder joint when seated with arms at sides
Expect shoulder joint to move during the exercise; alignment should reflect “average” midpoint of this excursion
Arm pad setting: choose Wide (W), Middle (M), or Narrow (N) based on torso width; most clients will likely use Middle; pads should be as narrow as possible to accommodate a narrow arm path into sides of torso
Elbows should land at least halfway down the arm pads and maintain a 90-degree angle throughout movement
Care must be given to bringing movement arm and arm pads down over the client; client may have to maneuver one arm at a time through pads
Standard lower turnaround gap is 6; always evaluate range of motion individually for safety
Reassess ROM if the instructor anticipates excessive range during first eccentric
Adjust settings if elbows slide or bend/straighten beyond 90 degrees during motion; this is the practical alignment requirement
Posture/Get Set
Establish a posterior pelvic tilt (PPT) to contract abdomen and create a rounded back posture
Avoid chest-up, arched-back posture and avoid pushing down with the hands on the movement arm
Pull movement arm over client with care, maneuvering arms between pads
Grab movement arm further from the axis for leverage during assist
Perform handoff with verbal cue (e.g. “driving back through your elbow, tighten your abdomen, that…is…yours”)
Ensure client establishes stillness and control before starting eccentric
Do not release full weight during handoff until client can maintain control
Cue increased force if movement occurs during handoff
Execution, Instruction, and Turnarounds
Exercise begins in the contracted position (upper turnaround) with instructor assist
Use PPT and elbow-driven effort to reinforce desired muscular feeling
Cue client to “slowly begin to allow the movement arm to rise…keeping the pressure through the elbows” during first eccentric
Before lower turnaround, cue client to “continuously,” “seamlessly,” or “immediately” reverse direction by tightening abdomen and driving through elbows
Clarify that “immediate” does not mean “sudden”
Upper turnaround occurs when elbows are fully drawn into the sides and midsection is tightened
Instruct against pushing arm further just to lift the stack higher; emphasize muscular objective…elbows in, abdomen tight
Trainer clicks only after confirming full ROM and control in contracted position
Clients often anticipate a click to signal when to stop, but it must come after they stop
Trainer must assertively control timing and prevent client from overshooting ROM
Cue concentric movement with “draw the elbows down into the ribs”
Note: posture changes between phases – ribcage inevitably rises (anterior pelvic tilt) during eccentric and lowers (PPT) during concentric
Intensity
Set ends when subject reaches concentric failure with perfect form
Only full reps are recorded on the chart
Partial reps beyond midpoint may be included when form is excellent and client is sufficiently experienced and comfortable with higher intensities
Newer or deconditioned clients should focus on full reps to moderate fatigue
Always progress client’s understanding of effort and intensity across sessions
Effort must voluntarily increase to counteract fatigue and maintain pace, this is how to generate mechanical tension
Notes
Standard lower turnaround gap of 6 is generally conservative; confirm gap setting for each machine individually with Studio Leader and MSF Corp
Use of a selector pin near the upper turnaround can provide a concentric end stop
Static Hold or Timed Static Contraction versions of this exercise may be used for safety or instructional purposes
Pullover engages nearly all upper body muscles and is among the most demanding exercises in the studio when properly loaded and performed
During early sessions, lighter weights often allow for more reps and longer time under load, which is necessary for learning
Be cautious that prolonged sets with light weight do not lead to intolerable discomfort before adequate stimulus as subject progresses
Strive for perfect form using “faster” tempo (6 sec concentric, 6 sec eccentric) with heavier weights targeting failure in ≤10 reps
Increase intensity progressively once client demonstrates consistent control and understanding
