Torso Rotation – Quick Reference Guide
This is an abbreviated version of the understanding required of a MSF Exercise Practitioner.  Please see Comprehensive Overview for more details.
Target Muscles
External Obliques – spinal rotation
Internal Obliques – spinal rotation
Synergists
Rotatores – spinal rotation
Hip Abductors – stabilize via hip horizontal abduction
Rectus Abdominis – spinal flexion / posterior pelvic tilt support
Setup
Seat Position
Options:
Positions 1 & 5 = largest rotation (most clients)
Positions 2 & 4 = reduced rotation (deconditioned or limited mobility)
Position 3 = neutral (not used for working sets)
Ensure seat is fully locked into stopper
Contraindication
Osteoporosis/Osteopenia:
No dynamic rotation
Use Static Hold only if medically cleared
Leg Pads
Thin pads = standard
Thick pads = smaller clients or to increase stretch
Mixed pads possible but generally unnecessary
Arm Pads
Position 1 (default): lowest and closest (most clients)
Position 4: higher for taller clients while staying close to torso
Other positions only for wider clients needing more space
Entry & Safety
Warn client: “Watch your head” (overhead workbox)
Posture / Get Set
Pelvis fully back into seat and hip pads
Use seat belt if pelvis cannot stay stable
Knees abducted into pads, feet flat under knees
Use blocks if needed for shorter clients
Arms wrap over pads, forearms along pad, hands “cap” ends
Posterior pelvic tilt (“tightened down”)
Mid-back stays in contact with pad (rounded spine)
Execution
Load-Up (Critical Teaching Phase)
Cue: “Tighten/crunch down”
Maintain mid-back contact
Sequence:
Draw far-side roller into body
Then rotate ribcage
Goal: attach carriage to ribcage before turning
Concentric (Rotation Phase)
Maintain posterior pelvic tilt (do not lift chest)
Emphasize:
Compact movement = more tension
Not chasing ROM
Stop when:
Obliques fully tightened (no further contraction possible)
Monitor:
Hips stay back in pads
Knees don’t slide forward
Head:
Follows torso or stays neutral
Never leads the movement
Arms:
Do not create extra movement beyond ribcage
Upper Turnaround
Brief pause every rep
From rep 3+: 2–3 second squeeze
Purpose:
Increase safety and control
Improve muscle awareness
Coaching must be specific:
“Keep tight, keep breathing… ease out”
Not just “squeeze”
Eccentric (Return Phase)
Cue: “Ease out”
Lower body remains completely still
Rotate only through ribcage
Maintain tension while returning
Cue before bottom:
“Only go far enough for weights to barely touch”
Lower Turnaround
At weight stack touch:
Immediate but smooth direction change
No pause:
Cue: “Drag through the bottom”
Key Technique Principles
Movement is driven by ribcage, not arms
Shorter ROM with higher tension is preferred
Stability of pelvis = better oblique loading
Avoid “reaching” or “turning as far as possible”
Programming Considerations
Train both directions
Reps: 4–7 per side (since exercise is doubled)
Avoid failure on first side
Rest enough to match reps on both sides
Final rep of first side: challenging but not failure
Special Population: Osteoporosis / Osteopenia
Default Approach
Avoid rotation entirely unless cleared
Static Hold (SH) Option (if cleared)
Seat at position 2 or 4
Carriage aligned square to torso
No movement at any time
Execution
Client braces and tightens midsection
Instructor performs interpersonal handoff
Hold: 20–60 seconds
Instructor controls load during unload
Key Rule
Zero movement during entire SH
Purpose: train safely without contraindicated rotation
Final Coaching Emphasis
Teach how to create tension, not just to move
Reinforce posture and control continuously
The goal is mechanical tension in the obliques, not maximal rotation or fatigue
